**Phase mirror dissonance:**

-   PhaseMirror claims CRMF is \"one and the same as CHL.\" The MOU R3
    > documents it as a licensed IP asset under a springing assignment,
    > not yet formally assigned --- those are structurally different
    > things.[^[\[1\]]{.underline}^](#rkmj4nxxcdd8)[^[\[2\]]{.underline}^](#f2st1dg3pz28)

-   The moat argument is correct in technical substance. The investor
    > concern is legal form, not technical uniqueness --- CRMF\'s
    > defensibility score (cited as 9.2/10) is not in
    > dispute.[^[\[2\]]{.underline}^](#f2st1dg3pz28)

-   PhaseMirror equates using PIRTM with using a dictionary. DNAKEY\'s
    > objection is not about the tool\'s value --- it is about what
    > ownership structure an examiner and investor sees when they look
    > at the stack.[^[\[1\]]{.underline}^](#rkmj4nxxcdd8)

-   DNAKEY\'s \"weight\" framing refers to the Springing Assignment
    > triggers 1 and 2 (Series A ≥\$5M; Revenue ≥\$2M ARR). Until those
    > fire, Proof/PIRTM/OLHES are exclusive-licensed, not owned. That
    > encumbrance is real and
    > documentable.[^[\[2\]]{.underline}^](#f2st1dg3pz28)[^[\[1\]]{.underline}^](#rkmj4nxxcdd8)

-   The \"15 years to replicate\" argument is a moat claim. It is not an
    > ownership claim. Investors and examiners evaluate both
    > separately.[^[\[2\]]{.underline}^](#f2st1dg3pz28)

-   The risk DNAKEY names --- \"if I found this train of thoughts it can
    > destroy CHL\" --- is a disclosure/prior art risk, not a personal
    > threat. PhaseMirror is not hearing that distinction.

-   Both parties agree on CRMF\'s value. The dispute is whether that
    > value needs formal IP scaffolding to survive institutional
    > scrutiny. It does.

**Levers to test now:**

-   \[DNAKEY / CHL Legal\] --- Model two scenarios: CHL IP stack with
    > CRMF carrying full springing-assignment weight (Triggers 1+2
    > intact) vs. CRMF treated as internally-generated CHL IP via
    > work-for-hire --- compare investor due diligence risk and patent
    > examiner abstraction exposure for each --- Metric: written legal
    > memo with risk delta per scenario --- Horizon: 7
    > days[^[\[1\]]{.underline}^](#rkmj4nxxcdd8)[^[\[2\]]{.underline}^](#f2st1dg3pz28)

-   \[Both Principals\] --- Separate the technical moat argument from
    > the legal ownership argument in writing --- produce a one-page
    > internal doc that states: (1) what CRMF is technically, (2) who
    > owns it under current executed agreements, (3) what triggers full
    > clean title --- Metric: both parties sign off --- Horizon: 48
    > hours[^[\[1\]]{.underline}^](#rkmj4nxxcdd8)

-   \[DNAKEY / Patent Counsel\] --- Map PIRTM\'s role explicitly: is it
    > prior art (public, open-source, non-encumbering) or is it a
    > licensed input to CRMF that creates claim dependency? This
    > determines whether the USPTO examiner sees abstraction risk or a
    > concrete method combination --- Metric: patent counsel memo
    > distinguishing PIRTM\'s role in claim construction --- Horizon: 5
    > days[^[\[3\]]{.underline}^](#b40hsjutj8x)[^[\[2\]]{.underline}^](#f2st1dg3pz28)

-   \[PhaseMirror\] --- Reframe the investor pitch: CRMF is the
    > application; PIRTM is the verifiable mathematical substrate. That
    > framing addresses both the \"not prior art\" concern and the \"not
    > abstract\" concern without conflating ownership with utility ---
    > Metric: investor one-pager drafted and reviewed by DNAKEY ---
    > Horizon: 3 days[^[\[2\]]{.underline}^](#f2st1dg3pz28)
