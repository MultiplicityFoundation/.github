**lets explore a business strategy for the Phase Mirror.**

Based on my comprehensive review of your Phase Mirror materials, I can
now provide a strategic business analysis.

**Business Strategy for the Phase Mirror**

The Phase Mirror has a well-developed technical foundation and licensing
structure. The key strategic question is **how to convert this into
sustainable revenue while maintaining adoption momentum**.

**Positioning: Source-Available Governance Infrastructure**

Your positioning should be explicit: the Phase Mirror is
**source-available infrastructure for managing productive contradictions
in AI systems**, not traditional open-source software. This framing
avoids the \"not real open source\" criticism while accurately
describing what you\'re offering.[^[\[1\]]{.underline}^](#fn1)

**Revenue Model Options**

  ------------------------- ---------------------------------------------------- ------------------------------------ -------------------
  Model                     Primary Revenue                                      Moat Durability                      Adoption Friction
  Open-core + SaaS          Hosted oracle, premium rules                         High (ops burden, network effects)   Low
  Certification + support   Enterprise support SLAs, certified implementations   Medium (trust, expertise)            Low
  FP calibration network    Aggregate false-positive data service                High (data network effects)          Medium
  ------------------------- ---------------------------------------------------- ------------------------------------ -------------------

The **FP calibration service** has the strongest moat because it
requires cross-customer data to improve recommendations---something
competitors can\'t replicate without similar adoption. Combined with a
hosted SaaS oracle, this creates defensible
differentiation.[^[\[1\]]{.underline}^](#fn1)

**Open Core / Proprietary Division**

**Open Core (Apache 2.0 equivalent with Phase Mirror License):**

-   Core oracle engine, decision logic, CLI

-   Tier A rules (MD-001 through MD-005): fast checks with near-zero
    false positives

-   Full redaction framework (safety-critical, must be inspectable)

-   Schema and NoOp implementations for local development

**Proprietary:**

-   Tier B rules (semantic analysis, cross-repo drift, compliance packs)

-   Hosted oracle SaaS with SLAs

-   Multi-repo federation for enterprise-wide aggregation

-   FP calibration service with cross-customer data
    pooling[^[\[1\]]{.underline}^](#fn1)

**Go-to-Market Sequence**

1.  **Immediate (0--30 days)**: Publish OSS core to GitHub, form Proof
    Labs LLC (Delaware), file trademark applications for \"Phase
    Mirror,\" \"QAGI,\" and \"Mirror
    Dissonance\"[^[\[1\]]{.underline}^](#fn1)

2.  **Near-term (30--90 days)**: Ship CLI + GitHub Actions integration,
    identify 3 enterprise pilot candidates for LOIs

3.  **Medium-term (90--180 days)**: Launch hosted SaaS in private beta,
    implement FP calibration consent mechanism

4.  **Long-term (180+ days)**: Transfer trademarks to Citizen Gardens
    Foundation per succession triggers, scale enterprise adoption

**Entity Structure**

The recommended structure follows the **Mozilla model**: Proof Labs LLC
holds trademarks initially, with binding commitment to transfer to a
501(c)(3) foundation (Citizen Gardens Foundation) within 24 months or
upon reaching adoption milestones. This balances commercial agility with
long-term stewardship.[^[\[1\]]{.underline}^](#fn1)

**Precision Question**

What\'s your primary monetization goal---**enterprise seats**
(compliance packs, support contracts), **usage-based SaaS** (hosted
oracle), or **certification revenue** (certified implementations)? This
determines whether compliance packs stay proprietary or become OSS with
paid
