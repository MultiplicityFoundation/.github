**Multiplicity**: (In mathematics) the **multiplicity** of a member of
a multiset is the number of times it appears in the multiset. For
example, the number of times a given polynomial equation has a root at a
given point aka. prime factor.

**Social: **Relating to the interaction of members in a community.

**Physics: **The science that deals with matter, energy, motion, and force.

**Social Physics** is a field of physics that studies society as an
isolated system made up of a diversity of members with prime factors of
interaction. It\'s focus is to appreciate the diversity of members and
their communities and identify what is held of most common value between
them and the processes by which their arrangements are configured and
how they change. It defines and appreciates various levels of
interaction with an emphasis on the most crucial and beneficial
interactions and their overall long term effects on the greater
community.

**Social** **Physics** uses **Multiplicity** to distinguish levels of
reciprocity between same energy members who only differ in their
reciprocity angle and momentum.  It is defined
as **2*R+1****, *where* R *is their reciprocity momentum*.*

**\"Set\" **a system that appreciates a single common value based on the
classification of it\'s lowest energy.\
**\"Multiset\" **a system that identifies and appreciates intrinsic
value at primary instances.

Social Physics is a \"**multiset**\" as it is both the qualification of
reciprocity between paired members and the quantification of the
potential reciprocity of unpaired members. Multiplicity is a result
of [[Hund\'s
rule]{.underline}](http://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hund%27s_rule) which
favors the single filling of same energy members. The result is the
filling of multiple members with a central reciprocity in multiplicity.
Multiplicity as defined in social physics is calculated using the
expression **2*R*+1**, where *R* = Σmr or, more simply put, ***R* =
-½**(the \# of members). When an individual member is paired to
a **set** *R=*0, and their value of multiplicity is **2(0)+1=1**, this
case is called a singlet. If a single member is paired to
a **multiset** ***R* = +½ **and **2(.5)+1=2**, which is called a
doublet. Two paired members in a \"multiset\" ***R=1 **r*esulting in a
triplet, etc.

**Hund\'s** **Rule** of maximum multiplicity in the context of social
physics is an observational rule which states that a greater
reciprocating state makes the resulting community more stable.
Accordingly, it can be taken that if two or more members of equal energy
are available, multiplicity occupies them individually before filling
them in pairs. The rule, discovered by Friedrich Hund in 1925, is of
important use in atomic chemistry, spectroscopy, quantum chemistry, and
now in the context of social physics. As a result this rule is often
abbreviated to Hunds\' Rule (ignoring Hunds\' other two rules).

Hund\'s three rules (in the context of social physics):

1\. For any given social configuration, the term with maximum
multiplicity takes the lowest energy. The reciprocity of which is equal
to the total multiplicity of all members combined. Therefore, the term
with lowest energy is also the term with highest value.

2\. For a given multiplicity, the term with the largest reciprocity has
the lowest energy.

3\. For a given term, when society is a \"set\" and members unpaired,
the level with the lowest value (for the operator ) lies lowest in
energy. If the society is paired, the level with the greatest value is
the lowest in energy.

These rules specify in a simple way how primary interactions dictate
terms of community. The rules assume that the repulsion between the
paired and unpaired members is of the greatest value in generating
reciprocity momentum.

**The Problem: **Our current economic society is a \"**set**\" as it
only identifies it\'s primary value through the focus of one distinct
physical element which bases its\' common value according to it\'s
lowest energy. Whenever a \"**set**\" (as opposed to **multiset**) is
formed, multiplicity is automatically ignored, without requiring use of
the term \"distinct\". 

                     **\"Set\"** = Unpaired: having a reciprocity
momentum of (0) with a value of 1 in multiplicity: 2(0)+1=1

**The Solution** a paired community (**multiset**) is a system that
doesn\'t just identify the primary factors between its members it seeks
to accommodate the member in their diversity to create a central
reciprocity of multiplicity. The notion of multiplicity is important to
be able to count correctly without specifying exceptions (for
example, *double *roots counted twice). Hence the expression, \"counted
with multiplicity\".\

                                **\"Multiset\"** = Paired: having a
reciprocity momentum of (.5) with a value of 2 in multiplicity:
2(.5)+1=2
