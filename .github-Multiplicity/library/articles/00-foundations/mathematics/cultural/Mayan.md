### Expanding Mayan Mathematics with Multiplicity Theory

Mayan mathematics, a sophisticated system developed in Mesoamerica, is
notable for its base-20 (vigesimal) number system, zero as a
mathematical concept, modular arithmetic for calendrical cycles, and
recursive astronomical modeling. By integrating **Mayan mathematics**
with **Multiplicity Theory**---a quantum-inspired framework that
emphasizes interconnectedness, recursion, and harmonic systems---we can
develop new methodologies for computation, cryptography, and modeling
dynamic systems.

### Core Concepts of Mayan Mathematics

1.  **Base-20 Number System**:

    -   The Mayans used a vigesimal system, with place values increasing
        > by powers of 20.

    -   This system accommodated both integer and fractional
        > representations.

2.  **Concept of Zero**:

    -   Among the first civilizations to independently conceptualize
        > zero as a placeholder and number, enabling advanced
        > computations.

3.  **Calendrical Systems**:

    -   The Mayan calendar combined modular arithmetic and harmonic
        > cycles, particularly the **Tzolk\'in (260-day calendar)** and
        > **Haab\' (365-day calendar)**.

4.  **Geometry and Astronomy**:

    -   Mayans used geometry to model celestial phenomena, align
        > architecture with astronomical events, and calculate ratios
        > and proportions.

5.  **Recursive Cycles**:

    -   Periodicity and recursion were central to Mayan systems,
        > reflecting their understanding of dynamic cycles in nature and
        > time.
