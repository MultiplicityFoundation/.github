Issue 27: Be D.Y.S.T.I.N.C.T My Journey with Dyslexia \| Ela Jamosmos

Ela traces her lifelong journey with undiagnosed dyslexia from silent
childhood struggles to creative breakthroughs and leadership, ultimately
transforming shame into self-acceptance and using her voice, art, and
public speaking to empower others to embrace their difference and own
their strengths.

Kindergarten graduation was approaching fast. There was only a short
time left to rehearse our program. One by one, we lined up at the edge
of the stage, facing rows of empty chairs, seats that would soon be
filled with proud parents.

Each of us was assigned a letter of the alphabet, starting with \"A\" on
the far left. There needed to be at least 26 of us to complete the full
set. I was given the letter \"M\" - the 13th letter in the alphabet.
Smack dab in the middle of the stage.

The plan was simple: we would sing the alphabet, each student taking a
turn to sing their letter in order. The moment the letter \"A\" was
sung, panic surged through me. I couldn\'t remember my letter.

Why can\'t I remember?

The rhythm broke. I froze. The melody stalled, and all I could hear was
the pounding of my heart, loud and fast in my tiny chest. Everything
else faded into a blur - the other kids, the teacher\'s face, even the
music. I knew every eye was on me, and I stood there, swallowed by
silence and shame.

I was five years old.

In the days that followed, I became obsessed with remembering. I
repeated \"M, M, M, M\" over and over to myself, day and night. During
practice, I waited for the student to my left to sing \"L,\" and I would
blurt out \"M\", not musically, not cheerfully, but with a stern
seriousness. My face must have looked terrified.

On graduation day, I did the same thing. One strong, flat \"M.\" No
melody, no joy, just survival. That moment marked the beginning of my
awareness that school and learning would always feel different for me.
The pressure made it difficult. Harder. Heavier. Not wrong... just
different.

Yes, different.

I remember a time when my mom was helping my older sister memorize a
song for school. They sat together, going over it again and again, while
I simply watched from the side. No one asked me to learn it. There was
no pressure, no expectation. I was just there... listening, enjoying,
soaking it in.

After their tutorial session, I surprised them both; I had memorised the
entire song. No stress. No flashcards. Just fun. That moment showed me
something I wouldn\'t fully understand until years later: I learn
differently. When learning feels like a performance, a play, or a
passion, I thrive. When it feels like a test, a trap, or a ticking
clock, I freeze.

I didn\'t lack ability. I learned with a different rhythm.\

Reinvention is the inevitable result of growth in leadership.

I didn't pursue leadership the way some people do---chasing titles or
trying to "become" a leader. For me, leadership became the natural
outcome of becoming someone who could no longer stay the same.

As I grew as a person---and later as a speaker---I realized there was no
forward path that didn't require courage, responsibility, and ownership.
Whether you carry a title or not, growth eventually asks you to lead.

With greater growth comes greater opportunity---and a wider platform to
serve beyond the spaces we already know. What began as leadership at
home and within my circle of influence naturally expanded outward.

My lived experience of this began when I took the risk of learning how
to speak publicly---one of the things I feared most. But as I grew as a
speaker, it inevitably gave me confidence to lead.

First, I found my voice.

Then it built my confidence.

And leadership was inevitable.

That journey was shaped through Toastmasters International, and their
tagline truly makes sense: "Where leaders are made."

Not just speakers, but leaders.

Becoming a stronger communicator changes you.

Not all great speakers are great leaders ---

but, most great leaders are great speakers.

That's why I personally believe that Toastmasters International is an
effective platform for leadership development---because it doesn't just
teach performance. It strengthens presence, clarity, and the courage to
show up consistently.

💬 Insights from lived experience on reinvention:

• Face your current reality so you can name exactly what needs to grow.

• Hold a clear vision of who you are becoming---not compared to others,
but compared to where you are now.

• Take steps toward that vision, no matter how small, consistently and
with unwavering determination.

Reinvention happens at every stage where we choose to grow.

Because reinvention is not becoming someone else---

it's becoming more of who you were always meant to be.

At the core of my work is a truth I've lived:

Many times we cannot change nor choose our circumstances.

But we can choose who we become within them.
