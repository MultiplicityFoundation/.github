Title

Multiplicity Theory in Sourdough Fermentation: A Prime-Encoded Approach
to Optimize Recipe Outcomes

Abstract

This research applies Multiplicity Theory to optimize a sourdough recipe
by encoding each ingredient as a unique quantum prime qubit,
facilitating computational simulations of the fermentation process. By
analyzing interactions among ingredients through multiplicative gates,
we aim to achieve precise control over the microbial ecosystem, dough
texture, and final flavor profile. This method demonstrates the
potential for quantum-assisted culinary optimization.

1\. Introduction

Sourdough bread relies on complex microbial interactions and
fermentation chemistry, making it an ideal candidate for analysis under
Multiplicity Theory. In this study, we apply principles of prime
encoding to capture the multiplicative relationships among ingredients
(flour, water, salt, and starter cultures). By encoding these components
into a quantum system, we explore a novel approach to optimizing
fermentation time, microbial balance, and baking outcomes.

3\. Results

Simulations suggest that prime-encoded modeling can pinpoint optimal
ingredient ratios and fermentation conditions. By applying specific
prime gate operations to hydration and microbial states, we achieved
optimal texture and flavor in simulated bakes, with reduced sourness and
enhanced elasticity.

4\. Conclusion

This study demonstrates the feasibility of Multiplicity Theory in
culinary applications. Encoding ingredients as prime qubits in quantum
simulations enables precise manipulation of dough fermentation,
showcasing the interdisciplinary potential of Multiplicity Theory. This
approach opens avenues for optimized recipes across diverse culinary
applications, where ingredient interaction complexity benefits from
quantum modeling.
