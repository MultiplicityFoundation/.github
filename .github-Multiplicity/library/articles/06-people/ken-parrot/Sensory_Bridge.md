1    Introduction
Sensory Awareness as the Neural Bridge Between Focus and Cognition **Au-
thor:** Kenneth Parrott
    Abstract This paper proposes that sensory awareness serves as the criti-
cal bridge linking focus and cognition, enabling the brain to transition fluidly
between deep attention and higher-order executive thinking. By regulating sen-
sory input through the integration of interoception and exteroception, sensory
awareness minimizes cognitive stressors, declutters working memory, and frees
cognitive bandwidth for adaptive and creative thought. This process is espe-
cially relevant to individuals with neurodiverse sensory profiles, where deliberate
training of sensory awareness can yield significant improvements in regulation,
performance, and well-being.
    —
    1. Introduction Focus and cognition are often treated as separate do-
mains—one concerning sustained attention, the other encompassing reasoning,
problem-solving, and creativity. However, these capacities are deeply interde-
pendent, and both are influenced by the efficiency with which the brain manages
incoming sensory data. Sensory awareness, as the skill of consciously and uncon-
sciously balancing internal and external sensory signals, provides the necessary
equilibrium for optimal functioning (Craig, 2009; Simmons et al., 2013).
    —
    2. Order of Necessity The relationship between sensory awareness, focus,
and cognition can be sequenced as follows:
    1. **Sensory Awareness (Baseline Regulation)**         - Integrates interocep-
tive and exteroceptive signals via the insula and salience network, acting as a
gatekeeper (Craig, 2009; Menon, 2015; Simmons et al., 2013).           - Minimizes
sensory stressors by engaging sensory gating in the thalamus to lay groundwork
for balance (Wilson et al., 2008).
    2. **Focus (Directed Attention)**         - Enhanced sensory balance allows
attentional systems (like the RAS and salience network) to maintain engagement
without intrusion (Barrett, 2017).
    3. **Cognition (Adaptive Processing)**            - With sensory clutter re-
duced, prefrontal networks and default mode systems can operate at full ca-
pacity—enabling planning, creativity, and self-reflection (Barrett, 2017).

    —
    3. Mechanistic Model - **Sensory Awareness as a Gatekeeper:**              The
insula plays a dual role in merging internal state and external context into a
salience-driven awareness that guides attention and executive control (Molnar-
Szakacs, 2022; Zhang et al., 2024).
    - **Stress Reduction via Allostatic Load Management:** Balancing sensory
input protects against chronic allostatic load, preventing the cognitive erosion
caused by sustained stress (Santamarı́a-Garcı́a et al., 2025; McEwen, 1998).
    - **Cognitive Decluttering via Sensory Gating:**         Efficient sensory gat-
ing—filtering out irrelevant stimuli—protects working memory and cognitive
throughput, especially under stress (Spooner et al., 2021).
    —
    4. Empirical Support - *Interoceptive processing*, critical to self-awareness
and emotion regulation, has been linked to attentional and cognitive function
in both autistic and ADHD populations (ResearchGate, 2012).
    - *Allostatic interoceptive overload* predicts vulnerability across neurodevel-
opmental and psychiatric conditions, underscoring the need for sensory balance
(Santamarı́a-Garcı́a et al., 2025).
    - *Stress-induced deficits in sensory gating* correlate with declines in global
cognition, highlighting how unmanaged sensory input undermines cognitive ca-
pacity (Spooner et al., 2021).
    —
    5. Implications for Neurodiverse Profiles For autistic and ADHD individu-
als, sensory awareness is not merely helpful—it is foundational. When sensory
regulation precedes task engagement: - Deep focus becomes more adaptable
and less isolating. - Cognitive switching becomes smoother and less taxing. -
Emotional regulation improves, due to fewer sensory-triggered disruptions.
    —
    6. Conclusion Sensory awareness isn’t an optional support — it’s the **es-
sential precondition** for sustained focus and productive cognition. By train-
ing sensory awareness first, individuals create a stable neural environment that
supports high-level thinking without overload. This model offers a roadmap for
intervention: Begin with sensory integration, then allow focused attention and
cognition to flourish.
