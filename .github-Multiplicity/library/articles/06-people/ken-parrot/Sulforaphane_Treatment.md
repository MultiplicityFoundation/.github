                     Sulforaphane Treatment
                                  Ken Parrott
                                September 2025


1    Introduction
[12pt]article geometry margin=1in setspace hyperref
   Sulforaphane-Primed Natural Learning:
A Supported Framework to Enhance Exteroception and Social Connectivity in
Autism Kenneth Parrott 2025

                                     Abstract
         This paper proposes a combined biological-educational method for
     autistic learners—particularly those with limited speech—by pairing sul-
     foraphane (SFN) supplementation with a naturalistic teaching paradigm
     focused on exteroception, sensory regulation, and guided inquiry. Evi-
     dence from randomized trials and mechanistic studies indicates SFN can
     improve behavioral outcomes and brain redox status, while sensory inte-
     gration, nature-based exposure, and guided inquiry show benefits for at-
     tention, regulation, and social participation. Together, these layers may
     increase connectivity (biological and social) by aligning internal physiol-
     ogy with experiential learning.


2    Evidence Base: Sulforaphane
Clinical Outcomes
In a double-blind RCT of young men with moderate–severe autism (n = 44),
daily SFN (50–150 µmol) for 18 weeks improved Aberrant Behavior Checklist
(34%) and Social Responsiveness Scale (17%); gains waned after discontinuation
[1].

Pediatric Trial
A 36-week randomized controlled study in children (ages 3–12) found caregiver-
rated improvements on the ABC and biomarker changes (glutathione redox,
mitochondrial respiration, inflammatory markers); SFN was well tolerated [2].




                                         1
Meta-Analytic Signal
Recent systematic reviews report overall symptom improvement with SFN across
trials, with longer durations showing stronger effects [3].

Mechanism and Brain Chemistry
SFN activates the KEAP1–NRF2 cytoprotective pathway and augments brain
glutathione; a pilot MRS study showed increased thalamic GSH correlating with
peripheral changes, consistent with improved hub efficiency for sensory relay [4].


3     Evidence Base: Naturalistic Teaching
Sensory Integration
A randomized trial of Ayres Sensory Integration in autistic children improved
goal attainment, self-care, and socialization vs usual care; subsequent reviews
corroborate functional gains [5].

Nature-Based Exposure
Meta-analysis shows nature-based interventions associate with improved social
communication and behavioral regulation in autistic children; broader Attention
Restoration Theory literature demonstrates nature exposure improves directed
attention and reduces mental fatigue [6, 7].

Guided Inquiry
A meta-analysis of 72 studies finds guided inquiry with scaffolds/feedback out-
performs unguided inquiry and expository instruction—aligning with interest-
driven learning and autonomy [8].


4     Combined Method: SPNL
Layer A: Medical Screening and SFN Priming
    • Screen for contraindications and medications; consider a broccoli-sprout–derived
      glucoraphanin + myrosinase preparation.
    • Dose within trial ranges (e.g., 50–150 µmol/day SFN equivalents), monitor
      tolerance, and track biomarkers where feasible (GSH redox, inflammatory
      markers).




                                        2
Layer B: Exteroceptive Engagement
    • Daily “sensory walks” (20–40 min, 5–7 days/week): observe wind, leaf
      motion, ambient sound, texture exploration; pair with low-demand social
      bids (pointing, shared attention).

    • Rationale: nature exposure restores directed attention and reduces arousal,
      improving readiness for social connection.

Layer C: Sensory Integration OT
    • Individualized Ayres-based sessions (2–3 per week) targeting vestibular/proprioceptive
      modulation.
    • Use Goal Attainment Scaling to track participation and socialization out-
      comes.

Layer D: Guided Inquiry Micro-Lessons
    • Short, curiosity-driven activities around preferred topics.
    • Incorporate prompts, models, and immediate feedback rather than drills.

    • Embed peer interaction within shared tasks.

Layer E: Nature-Based Social Practice
    • Low-ratio outdoor groups (1–2 per week) using cooperative tasks (build,
      map, observe).
    • Emphasize turn-taking via shared sensory goals rather than compliance
      scripts.


5     Measurement Plan
    • Primary: ABC and SRS-2 at baseline and every 6–8 weeks.
    • Secondary: caregiver stress, participation scales, Goal Attainment Scal-
      ing.

    • Optional: blood GSH/GSSG; where available, MRS thalamic GSH as a
      connectivity-adjacent index.

6    Hypothesized Synergy
SFN lowers oxidative/inflammatory noise and improves cellular energetics (in-
cluding thalamic redox balance), priming neural circuits for sensory gating and
attention. Naturalistic, exteroception-forward teaching then channels this readi-
ness into shared attention, turn-taking, and communicative bids—converting
biological connectivity into social connectivity.


7    Safety and Practical Notes
SFN supplements vary in purity; use third-party–tested sources, involve a clin-
ician, and monitor for minor side effects (e.g., GI upset, taste/smell aversion).
The method is an adjunct—not a replacement—for educational supports and
family-centered care.


8    Conclusion
A supported pathway emerges: prime the brain’s redox and mitochondrial sys-
tems with SFN, then teach in ways that match human sensory design—exteroceptive,
curiosity-led, and scaffolded. The literature suggests each component works on
its own; integrating them offers a credible route to greater social connectivity
for autistic learners.
