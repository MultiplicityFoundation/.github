Prime-Indexed Multiplicity Cobordism Invariants
(PIMCI)
Canonicalization (“Kirby gauge”), smooth sensitivity, and a minimal
validation pipeline
Context. This document distills a checkable mathematical core for your prime-indexed cobordism
invariants idea in the Multiplicity / MOC style:


     • primes label operator families and “tiers” are extracted by idempotent projectors Πpr ,
     • cobordisms/handle moves act as noncommuting operator words,
     • adequacy can be evaluated by a resonance functional R ∈ [0, 1].

Where PIMCI becomes genuinely mathematical (and not presentation-dependent) is the canonicalization
step: invariants are computed only after reducing the chosen surgery/handle presentation to a canonical
representative of its Kirby/handle-equivalence class.




1. Core problem: handle slides act by congruence, not similarity
For a framed-link / 2-handle presentation producing an integral symmetric matrix K ∈ Mm (Z) (linking/
intersection data), a handle slide corresponds algebraically to


$$ K \longmapsto U K U^{\mathsf T},\qquad U\in GL(m,\mathbb Z), $$


where U is an elementary matrix.


Therefore, raw “spectral” quantities like Tr(K k ) are not invariants unless we first pass to a canonical
representative of the stable congruence class.




2. Kirby gauge: explicit generating moves and canonicalization
target

2.1 Gauge group generators (moves)

Start from a surgery/plumbing presentation P of a (homotopy) sphere Σn that yields an integral symmetric
matrix K .


Let the gauge group be generated by:


    1. Permutation / relabeling




                                                      1
$$ K\mapsto P K P^{\mathsf T},\quad P\ \text{a permutation matrix.} $$


    1. Orientation flip (reverse orientation of a component/handle)

$$ K\mapsto D K D,\quad D=\mathrm{diag}(\pm1,\ldots,\pm1). $$


    1. Handle slide i ← i + εj with ε ∈ {±1}

$$ K\mapsto U_{ij}(\varepsilon)\,K\,U_{ij}(\varepsilon)^{\mathsf T},\quad U_{ij}(\varepsilon)=I+\varepsilon
E_{ij}. $$


    1. Stabilization (Kirby I blow-up/down)

$$ K\mapsto K\oplus[\pm1] $$


(and the inverse move when a ±1 summand splits off).


These generate the standard equivalence of framed-link presentations relevant for computing stable
invariants.


2.2 Canonicalization target

Define the canonicalization target as the stable isometry class of the quadratic lattice


$$ (\mathbb Z^m,\,K)\quad\text{(+ optional refinements: spin/characteristic data, smooth defect, etc.).} $$


Two presentations are equivalent iff their lattices are related by the above moves (congruence +
stabilization + relabeling/orientation).




3. Practical canonicalization: do it prime-by-prime
A robust, computable canonical “name” for K can be produced using local classification:


Step A — normalize parity/spin constraints

If working in the spin/framed setting, stabilize (via ⊕[±1]) until the parity condition (even/odd,
characteristic element constraints) matches the intended bordism structure.


For each prime p, classify the p-adic completion up to GL(m, Zp )-congruence:


$$ K\otimes\mathbb Z_p\ \cong\ \bigoplus_{r\ge 0} p^{r}\,K_{p,r}, $$


with Kp,r unimodular over Zp .


Record the canonical symbol

Step C — the real place

Record σ(K ⊗ R) (signature) and parity; these constrain global reconstruction.

Optionally, reconstruct a canonical integral representative          Kcan from {NFp (K)}p and σ , using a
deterministic “lexicographically minimal” construction.


Output of canonicalization (“Kirby gauge”):


$$ \mathrm{Can}(\mathcal P)=\Big({\mathsf{NF}_p(K)}_p,\ \sigma,\ \text{parity/refinements}\Big). $$




Why you need this

Many invariants extracted from K alone are homotopy-level. To detect exotic smooth structure on
spheres, you must include a smooth-sensitive term.


Dimension 7: the Eells–Kuiper invariant

For homotopy 7-spheres, the renormalized Eells–Kuiper invariant               μ(Σ) ∈ Z/28 classifies oriented
diffeomorphism types (equivalently, it detects the 28 exotic smooth structures).


A minimal integration into PIMCI is to attach a scalar defect δ(Σ) = μ(Σ) and augment the multiplicity
matrix:


$$ \widetilde M_\Sigma=\begin{pmatrix}M_\Sigma & 0\ 0 & \delta(\Sigma)\end{pmatrix}. $$


Prime-primary structure becomes explicit because


$$ \mathbb Z/28\ \cong\ (\mathbb Z/4)\times(\mathbb Z/7), $$


so only primes 2 and 7 carry the classification signal at the smooth level.



Define Πpr on canonical symbols (not on a basis-dependent matrix):


$$ \Pi_{p^r}(\mathsf{NF}p(K)) := {(r,\mathrm{rank}(K),\ldots)}. $$

6. PIMCI invariants (canonical-symbol first; matrix-spectral
optional)

Canonical-symbol invariants (recommended)

For each prime power pr , define invariants directly from the canonical symbol and smooth defect:


      • block ranks rank(Kp,r ),
      • unit/Hasse-type data,
      • parity/characteristic data,
      • smooth defect components μ mod 4 and μ mod 7 in dimension 7.

Package as a prime-spectrum:


$$ \mathfrak I(\Sigma)=\big{\mathfrak I_{p^r}(\Sigma)\big}_{p,r}. $$


Optional spectral layer (if you insist on traces/determinants)

Once Kcan (or MΣ,can ) is fixed canonically:


$$ \mathfrak I_{p^r,k}(\Sigma)=\mathrm{Tr}\big((\Pi_{p^r}\widetilde M_{\Sigma,\mathrm{can}})^k\big),
\qquad \mathfrak J_{p^r}(\Sigma)=\det(I-s\,\Pi_{p^r}\widetilde M_{\Sigma,\mathrm{can}}). $$




7. Cobordisms as operator words + strict invariance via
canonicalization
Encode a cobordism W : Σ0 ⇝ Σ1 as an operator word W built from prime-indexed noncommuting
generators.


Instead of trying to force W-independence directly, enforce invariance by definition:


$$ \mathfrak I(\Sigma) := \mathfrak I\big(\mathrm{Can}(\mathcal P(\Sigma))\big). $$


Since Can quotients by Kirby/handle equivalence, I is presentation-independent.

8. Resonance functional as a distance on canonical data
Turn “resonance” into a mathematically strict notion:


      • Define a discrepancy d between canonical datasets,
      • set R = exp(−d) ∈ (0, 1],
      • then R = 1 iff canonical data match.

Example sketch:


$$ d(\Sigma_0,\Sigma_1)=\sum_p w_p\,d_p\big(\mathsf{NF}p(K_0),\mathsf{NF}_p(K_1)\big) + w\infty|\sigma_0-
\sigma_1| + w_\mu\,\mathbf 1[\mu_0\ne\mu_1]. $$




9. Minimal viable experiment (MVP) for validation: Milnor 7-spheres
Goal: verify (i) Kirby-move invariance and (ii) exotic sensitivity.


     1. Dataset. Choose explicit Milnor spheres Σ with known μ(Σ) values.


     2. Pipeline. For each Σ:


     3. parse plumbing/surgery data ⇒ K ,


     4. compute Can(P) ⇒ {NFp (K)}p , σ, parity,
     5. attach δ(Σ) = μ(Σ),

     6. compute Π2r , Π7r outputs.


     7. Kirby invariance tests. Randomly apply sequences of algebraic moves K ↦ U KU T and K ↦
       K ⊕ [±1], then confirm Can(P) (and therefore I) is unchanged.

     8. Exotic sensitivity. Check whether the extracted (2-primary, 7-primary) data correlates exactly
        with μ mod 4 and μ mod 7; if yes, PIMCI recovers the correct Z/28 splitting.


     9. Stretch goal. Move beyond spheres to 2-connected 7-manifolds where generalized invariants refine
        classification.
