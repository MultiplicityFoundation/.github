                                                    A BSTRACT
         Multiplicity Theory represents a unifying framework integrating principles from mathematics,
         physics, and computational sciences. By employing prime-based encoding, tensor networks,
         quantum-inspired optimization, and recursive feedback loops, it addresses challenges in quantum
         computing, astrophysics, and social systems. This paper provides a mathematical overview of the
         core concepts and discusses their interdisciplinary applications.




1   Introduction


Multiplicity Theory emerges as a versatile framework capable of bridging classical and quantum paradigms. It
leverages the inherent multiplicative structures of prime numbers, eigenvalues, and tensor products to provide solutions
to complex systems [1, 3]. By building on foundational theories like K-theory [2], string theory [5], and quantum
mechanics [4], this framework establishes a coherent structure for analyzing interconnected phenomena.




2   Mathematical Foundations


K-Theory classifies vector bundles over topological spaces. The Grothendieck group K(X) associated with a space X
is formed by equivalence classes of vector bundles, with the addition operation defined by the direct sum of bundles:

                                                K(X) = Vect(X)/ ∼,                                                   (1)

where ∼ denotes the equivalence relation induced by stable isomorphism.

    This foundation has been instrumental in understanding the topological invariants of spaces, with applications in
index theory and elliptic operators [2].
