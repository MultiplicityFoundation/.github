Substance P (SP) has been implicated in cancer development and
progression through several mechanisms. Its contribution to cancer is
significant in certain contexts, particularly due to its involvement in
inflammation, tumor growth, and metastasis. Here's an overview of how SP
contributes to cancers and its significance:

1\. Mechanisms of SP in Cancer Progression

Substance P, acting primarily through the neurokinin-1 receptor (NK1R),
influences cancer progression in several ways:

A. Cell Proliferation and Tumor Growth

• SP promotes the proliferation of cancer cells by binding to NK1R and
activating signaling pathways, such as:

• Mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) pathway

• Phosphoinositide 3-kinase (PI3K)/AKT pathway

• These pathways stimulate cell cycle progression and prevent apoptosis,
leading to tumor growth.

B. Angiogenesis

• SP promotes angiogenesis (formation of new blood vessels), which
supplies oxygen and nutrients to tumors, supporting their growth and
metastasis. It does so by:

• Stimulating vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) production.

• Enhancing endothelial cell migration and proliferation.

C. Inflammation

• Chronic inflammation is a key driver of cancer. SP is a potent
pro-inflammatory mediator and contributes to a tumor-promoting
inflammatory environment by:

• Recruiting immune cells like macrophages and neutrophils to the tumor
microenvironment.

• Promoting the release of inflammatory cytokines such as IL-1, IL-6,
and TNF-α.

D. Metastasis

• SP enhances the motility and invasiveness of cancer cells by:

• Activating cytoskeletal remodeling.

• Increasing the expression of matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs), which
degrade the extracellular matrix, facilitating cancer cell migration.

E. Drug Resistance

• SP-NK1R signaling is implicated in the development of resistance to
chemotherapy and targeted therapies by upregulating survival pathways in
cancer cells.

2\. Cancers Linked to Substance P

The role of SP has been documented in several cancers, including:

• Breast Cancer: Promotes proliferation, angiogenesis, and metastasis.

• Lung Cancer: Enhances tumor growth and invasiveness.

• Glioblastoma: Contributes to aggressive tumor behavior by stimulating
proliferation and angiogenesis.

• Colorectal Cancer: Associated with chronic inflammation and tumor
progression.

• Prostate Cancer: Involved in tumor growth and survival mechanisms.

• Pancreatic Cancer: Contributes to inflammation and cancer-associated
pain, which further drives tumor progression.

3\. Significance of SP in Cancer

The significance of SP in cancer depends on the cancer type and its
reliance on the SP-NK1R axis:

• Therapeutic Target: Blocking NK1R with antagonists (e.g., aprepitant,
a clinically approved NK1R antagonist) has shown promise in preclinical
studies for reducing tumor growth, angiogenesis, and metastasis.

• Prognostic Marker: Elevated SP or NK1R levels in tumors often
correlate with poor prognosis, advanced stages, and aggressive cancer
behavior.

4\. Preclinical and Clinical Insights

• Preclinical Studies:

• Blocking SP-NK1R signaling inhibits tumor growth and metastasis in
animal models.

• SP antagonists reduce VEGF production, limiting angiogenesis.

• Clinical Observations:

• Higher levels of SP and NK1R expression are observed in aggressive and
treatment-resistant cancers.

• Clinical trials are exploring NK1R antagonists for cancer therapy.

5\. Conclusion

Substance P significantly contributes to cancer progression through its
pro-inflammatory, pro-proliferative, and pro-angiogenic effects. Its
role in metastasis and drug resistance makes it a potential therapeutic
target. However, the extent of its contribution varies by cancer type,
