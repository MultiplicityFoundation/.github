PIMCI — Prime-Indexed Multiplicity Cobordism
Invariants
Abstract
We formalize a prime-indexed family of cobordism/diffeomorphism invariants intended to detect smooth
structure on homotopy spheres and related manifolds. The core idea is to attach to a manifold a Kirby-safe
algebraic package (an integral quadratic form together with a smooth refinement), reduce it to a
canonical normal form under the equivalence generated by handle/Kirby moves (stable integral
congruence + stabilization), and then extract prime-power layers via p-adic truncation projectors Πpr . A
multiplicity operator calculus viewpoint is incorporated by representing cobordisms as noncommuting
operator words acting on the carrier data, and by using a (soft) resonance functional as an adequacy
metric during computation while invariance is ultimately enforced by canonicalization.


         Status note. The canonicalization and prime-layer extraction are intended to be strict
         invariants once the normal form is fully specified. The resonance functional is optional and
         should be treated as a computational stability/fit score rather than the definition of
         equivalence.




1. Notation and setting

1.1 Bordism categories
            spin
Let Bordn denote the (symmetric monoidal) n-dimensional spin bordism category (variants: framed/
oriented). Objects are closed n-manifolds with the chosen structure; morphisms are bordisms.


1.2 The target data category

Define a category QuadRef whose objects are pairs


$$ K=(Q,\rho) $$


where:


     • Q is an integral symmetric bilinear form (matrix) representing an intersection form or surgery kernel
       (depending on dimension/model), and
     • ρ is a smooth refinement datum sufficient to reconstruct a smooth-sensitive invariant (e.g.
         Pontryagin class data, characteristic vectors, η/APS correction terms, or an Eells–Kuiper-type
         refinement).




                                                        1
Morphisms in QuadRef are generated by:


    1. Integral congruence (basis change)

$$ (Q,\rho)\mapsto (U^{\mathsf T} Q U,\; U^{\mathsf T}\rho)\quad (U\in GL(k,\mathbb Z)), $$


    1. Stabilization / blow-up

$$ (Q,\rho)\mapsto (Q\oplus[\pm 1],\;\rho\oplus\rho_0), $$


with the corresponding inverse move when the summand is recognized as cancellable/trivial in the chosen
theory.


We write K ∼ K ′ if they are related by the generated equivalence. This is the algebraic shadow of Kirby/
handle moves.




2. Data extraction from a manifold

2.1 Input presentations

For a manifold M (especially a homotopy sphere Σn ), choose one of:


     • a framed link / Kirby diagram,
     • a plumbing graph (e.g. for Milnor-type constructions),
     • a handle decomposition.

From this presentation, produce a bounding cobordism W (when available) that carries the relevant
structure (e.g. spin bounding for 7-spheres in the standard testbed).


Define


$$ K(M) := (Q_W,\rho_W)\in \mathbf{QuadRef}. $$


Typical choices:


     • QW : intersection form on a middle-dimensional homology group (e.g. H4 (W ; Z) for an 8-
         dimensional bounding W of a 7-manifold).
     • ρW : refinement encoding smooth structure (examples include characteristic vectors, Pontryagin
         class evaluations, and/or data needed to compute an Eells–Kuiper-type invariant).

         Design requirement (smooth sensitivity). ρW must not be purely homotopy/homology-
         level if the goal is to distinguish exotic smooth structures.




                                                          2
3. Canonicalization (the “gauge-fix”)

3.1 Canonical normal form

A canonicalization map is a function


$$ \mathsf{NF}: \mathbf{QuadRef}\to \mathcal K $$


into a set K of canonical representatives such that:


      • NF(K) = NF(K ′ ) whenever K ∼ K ′ , and
      • NF is deterministic (independent of choices once the input K is fixed).

Informally, NF is a complete invariant of the Kirby-generated equivalence on (Q, ρ) within the chosen class
of objects.


3.2 Implementable canonicalization scheme (algebraic)

A practical NF can be defined as a structured tuple of invariants and decompositions, for example:


      • rank, signature, parity, discriminant of Q,
      • local p-adic Jordan decompositions of Q ⊗ Zp for primes in scope,
      • a canonical representative of ρ modulo the automorphism group of Q (or relative to the p-adic
       decomposition).

       Key constraint. Many spectral quantities (trace powers/eigenvalues) are not invariants of
       integral congruence. Canonicalization must happen before spectral-style postprocessing.




4. Prime-tier projectors

4.1 p-adic truncation projectors

For each prime power pr , define a projector


$$ \Pi_{p^r}: \mathcal K\to \mathcal K_{p^r} $$


interpreted as “retain information visible modulo pr ” (or in Zp up to depth r ). Concretely, if NF(K) includes
p-adic Jordan blocks and refinement components, Πpr keeps:


      • the p-adic data of Q up to exponent r , and
      • the refinement ρ reduced/normalized modulo pr in a way compatible with the block decomposition.




                                                       3
4.2 Algebraic identities

The projectors satisfy:


      • idempotence: Π2pr = Πpr ,
      • filtration compatibility: Πpr ∘ Πpr+1 = Πpr ,
      • coprime independence (conceptually): distinct primes act on independent localizations (formalized
        as a product of local data in NF).




5. Multiplicity-weighted matrices (optional postprocessing)
Once NF(K(M )) is computed, one may define a multiplicity-weighted matrix representation


$$ M(M) := D\,Q^{\mathsf{NF}}\,D, $$


where:


      • QNF is the canonical form/matrix part extracted from NF(K(M )),
                     μ        μ
      • D = diag(p1 1 , … , pk k ) assigns primes pi and exponents μi deterministically from canonical
         block ordering.

This preserves invariance because prime assignment occurs after canonicalization.




6. Definition of PIMCI (prime-indexed invariants)

6.1 The invariant family

Define the Prime-Indexed Multiplicity Cobordism Invariants (PIMCI) of M by


$$ \mathfrak I_{p^r}(M) := F_{p^r}\big(\Pi_{p^r}(\mathsf{NF}(K(M)))\big), $$


where Fpr is any deterministic functional on the p-tier canonical data.


Examples of allowed functionals (once in canonical form):


      • invariants of the p-adic Jordan decomposition (block types, exponents, local signs),
      • invariants derived from the refinement ρ modulo pr ,
      • spectral-style quantities computed from canonical matrices M (M ) (trace powers, characteristic
         polynomials) only after canonicalization.

6.2 Aggregation over primes

Optionally define an aggregated invariant




                                                        4
$$ \mathfrak I(M) := {\mathfrak I_{p^r}(M)}{p\ \text{prime},\ r\le r. $$}(p)


In practice, only primes dividing the relevant classification torsion (e.g. those dividing ∣Θn ∣ for homotopy
spheres) contribute nontrivially in low dimensions.


6.3 Invariance statement (intended)

If M ≅ M ′ (diffeomorphic within the chosen structure) and the extraction K(M ) is compatible with
handle/Kirby calculus, then


$$ \mathfrak I_{p^r}(M)=\mathfrak I_{p^r}(M')\quad\text{for all }p^r. $$


Moreover, if M and M ′ are bordant and the theory is set up as a bordism functor, one expects Ipr to be
constant on the corresponding bordism classes (depending on the chosen ρ).




7. Cobordisms as noncommuting operator words (MOC integration)

7.1 Carrier space

Represent the evolving presentation data as a carrier


$$ \mathcal M=(X,H), $$


where H is a relational topology object (graph/hypergraph of generators and relations) and X holds state-
like data (forms/refinements, block decompositions, etc.).


7.2 Prime-indexed operators

For each prime p and tier r , define noncommuting operators


$$ \hat O^{(r)}_p: \mathcal M\to \mathcal M $$


that implement elementary transformations (slides, stabilizations, refinement updates) while tracking
prime-tier structure.


A cobordism (or a sequence of handle moves) corresponds to a word


$$ \mathsf W\in \langle \hat O^{(r)}_p\rangle, $$


acting by M ↦ W[M]. Noncommutativity reflects order-sensitivity of moves before reduction.


7.3 Strict invariance via reduction

Define the strict equivalence class by canonicalization:




                                                         5
$$ [\mathcal M] := \mathsf{NF}(K(M))\quad\text{(plus any additional canonical data).} $$


Then PIMCI is computed as a function of [M], ensuring move invariance.

A resonance functional


$$ R(H,{\hat O};D)\in[0,1] $$

Recommended role:


      • Guide search for canonical reductions (choose move sequences that increase R),
      • Detect numerical instability or incomplete reduction,
      • Provide a graded notion of “fit” before strict invariants are finalized.

Not recommended: defining equivalence of manifolds purely by maximizing R.




9. Specialization to 7-dimensional homotopy spheres (benchmark)

9.1 Known classification target

Let Θ7 be the group of oriented diffeomorphism classes of smooth homotopy 7-spheres under connected
sum. It is known that Θ7 ≅ Z/28. A classical smooth invariant (Eells–Kuiper μ) detects this structure in the
parallelizable-bounding setting and provides a concrete benchmark for any proposed invariant family.


9.2 Prime-spectrum prediction

Since 28 = 4 ⋅ 7, low-tier nontrivial prime data should concentrate at p = 2 and p = 7. Thus a first-pass
PIMCI for Θ7 should reproduce (or strongly correlate with) μ mod 4 and μ mod 7 through I2r and I7r at
small r .

11. Extensions and outlook
     • Beyond homotopy spheres: apply to inertia groups / 2-connected 7-manifolds where generalized μ-
       invariants appear.
     • Quantum gravity weighting: define weights w(M ) from prime-spectrum invariants (e.g. root-of-
       unity phases) for toy “sum over smoothings.”
     • String topology: interpret Πpr as p-local shadows of loop product/operations, potentially revealing
       smooth refinements not visible in homotopy type alone.
