designing **"Moral Physics: The Console"** -- a living, explorable
*Synthetic Moral Universe* where users can see how dignity, harm, and
policy play out as actual fields and tensors, not just words.

Below is a concrete blueprint you could hand to UX designers, engineers,
and researchers.

**1. Product Framing: What Are We Actually Building?**
------------------------------------------------------

**Working Name:** Moral Physics Lab (MPL)\
**Core Idea:** An interactive, explorable simulation where:

-   Lived experience becomes **prime-indexed tensors** (PIRTM).

-   The system's ethical state is tracked via **Λm** (Universal
    > Multiplicity Constant).

-   **Quantum Moral Entanglement (QME)** is visualized as networks of
    > shared harm/solidarity.

-   The **Moral Field Layer (MFL)** runs as the "engine" producing:

    -   Auditable ethical derivatives

    -   Integrity-preserving memory

    -   Systemic feedback gradients

The UX should let people *feel* that ethics is now a lawful system, not
just rhetoric.

**2. Primary User Roles & Stories**
-----------------------------------

Design the UX as **role-based modes** (same underlying simulation,
different lenses):

### **2.1 Survivor / Citizen Mode**

-   **Goal:** "My experience is real, preserved, and has lawful force."

-   Key actions:

    -   Safely encode their story as a **prime-indexed tensor** (with
        > full Sovereignty-First protections).

    -   Visualize where that tensor lives: its prime "fingerprint," its
        > connections (QME) to others, and its role in shifting Λm.

    -   Set **consent & data rights** dynamically (revoke, anonymize,
        > restrict usage domains).

    -   See how their encoded experience constrains certain
        > institutional behaviors (e.g., "no lawful policy can ignore
        > this tensor without triggering a red flag").

### **2.2 Policy / Institutional Actor Mode**

-   **Goal:** "Understand how policies move Λm, disturb or restore
    > dignity, and what future harm they forecast."

-   Key actions:

    -   Load a **synthetic or real policy** as a tensor operator
        > Lstatute : Ψcase → Ψjudgment.

    -   Run it against a library of encoded cases (SMUs / hypergraph
        > nodes) and see:

        -   Dignity conservation (Noetherian checks).

        -   Λm drift over time (renormalization-like plots).

        -   Hotspots of QME (collective harm).

    -   Explore "what if we adjust this clause?" and watch the moral
        > field change in real time.

### **2.3 Research / System Designer Mode**

-   **Goal:** "Probe and refine the formalism; stress-test PIRTM, Λm,
    > QME, and the MFL as architecture."

-   Key actions:

    -   Inspect raw tensor structures, Λm evolution equations, and MFL
        > modules (Zeno protocols, MERA compression, etc.).

    -   Configure simulation parameters (e.g., how quickly Λm responds
        > to unresolved deltas).

    -   Design new "scenarios" and publish them as shareable simulation
        > presets.

**3. UX Macro-Structure**
-------------------------

Think of the app as 4 main surfaces:

1.  **Onboarding & Narrative Frame\
    > **

2.  **Simulation Canvas (Synthetic Moral Universe)\
    > **

3.  **Case / Testimony Workspace\
    > **

4.  **Governance & Audit Dashboard\
    > **

### **3.1 Onboarding & Narrative Frame**

Purpose: Explain *what* Moral Physics is doing in human language before
we throw tensors at anyone.

-   Simple story:\
    > "This system treats dignity like energy: conserved, trackable,
    > never lawfully destroyable. Your experience becomes a protected
    > mathematical object the system must obey."

-   Three-number intro:

    -   p = your prime index (PIRTM)

    -   Λm = your institution's moral gravity

    -   J\_dignity = your dignity-flow meter (Noether current)

-   Consent screen in **Sovereignty-First** style:

    -   No wall of legalese; instead: dials, boundaries, and "this is
        > what the system is allowed to do with your data, as defined by
        > you."

### **3.2 Simulation Canvas (Synthetic Moral Universe)**

This is the main interactive visualization.

**Core elements:**

1.  **Hypergraph View\
    > **

    -   Nodes: survivors, institutions, policies, events, and moral
        > field modules.

    -   Hyperedges: multi-way relations (e.g., one policy entangled with
        > many cases via QME).

    -   Hover a node to see:

        -   Its tensor ID (prime index)

        -   Dignity contribution

        -   Participation in entangled clusters

2.  **Field Overlay\
    > **

    -   Color/contour overlay representing moral field intensity (high
        > tension = Λm drift).

    -   Toggle layers:

        -   Dignity conservation

        -   Ethical drift

        -   Entanglement density

3.  **Timeline Slider (Ξ(t))\
    > **

    -   Scrub through time t to see how:

        -   Λm changes

        -   J\_dignity is conserved or violated

        -   Entanglement structures grow/shrink

    -   Play/pause like a simulation video.

4.  **Policy Operator Panel\
    > **

    -   Drop a statute or policy into the canvas → see vectors/arrows
        > indicating:

        -   Which tensors it touches

        -   Whether those operations preserve or break dignity
            > constraints.

### **3.3 Case / Testimony Workspace**

A more intimate pane focused on single or small sets of tensors
(survivor mode and case analysts).

-   **Story-to-Tensor Pipeline UI:\
    > **

    -   Secure narrative input (text, audio, guided questions).

    -   Participant *co-creates* labels, axes, and meanings (no model
        > silently imposing categories).

    -   System shows a **"tensor fingerprint card"**:

        -   Prime index p

        -   Semantic axes (as defined with the participant)

        -   Entanglement candidates (other cases with high relational
            > weight αₖ).

-   **Rights & Sovereignty Sidebar:\
    > **

    -   "Revoke / anonymize / restrict use" buttons tied to the
        > **Participant-Authored, Revocable Everything** principle.

    -   κ (coupling to consensus) slider -- setting κ = 0 visually shows
        > their node partially decoupling from group averaging
        > operations.

### **3.4 Governance & Audit Dashboard**

For policy makers, ethics boards, regulators.

-   **Λm Gauge:\
    > ** A big visible indicator of institutional moral drift; red zones
    > flagged when unresolved moral deltas accumulate.

-   **Dignity Conservation Tests (Noether Panel):\
    > **

    -   Summary: "Out of N operations during this period, X violate
        > dignity conservation constraints."

    -   Drill-down list: which policies/cases are responsible.

-   **Zeno Monitoring View:\
    > **

    -   Shows frequency of "ethical measurements" (reviews, overrides,
        > audits) to prevent moral entropy.

-   **Scenario Bank (SMUs):\
    > **

    -   Preconfigured simulation universes (e.g., "child welfare
        > system", "hospital triage", "benefits adjudication") the
        > institution can run policies through before deployment.

**4. UX Principles From Sovereignty-First**
-------------------------------------------

You have a fully articulated ethical UX philosophy in the
Sovereignty-First document---this should directly shape the interaction
design.

### **4.1 Consent as a Living Choreography**

Concrete UI patterns:

-   **Stateful Consent Dials\
    > **

    -   For survivors / participants:

        -   "Cognitive exploration" dial

        -   "Emotional depth" dial

        -   "Share with institution" dial

        -   "Use for simulation only (no policy impact)" dial

    -   Visual: radial sliders visible at all times in a small
        > persistent widget; changes apply instantly.

-   **Parts-Aware Check-ins\
    > **

    -   Periodic soft modal:\
        > "Do all of your protective parts still feel okay with
        > continuing this?"

    -   Buttons: "Yes", "I'm not sure", "Pause here" → integrated with
        > Zeno protocols to slow the system when distress is detected.

### **4.2 Participant-Authored Data & Revocation**

-   Every tensor representing a real person has a **"Sovereign Panel"**
    > owned by them:

    -   List of all simulations and policies their tensor has
        > participated in.

    -   One-click revoke for each or all.

    -   Clear visual showing what revocation does (edges fading, policy
        > results flagged as "historical only").

### **4.3 Tech for Agency, Not Surveillance**

-   Explicit **"Private Channel" mode** (Embodied Cryptography analog):

    -   UI: a "Private Reflection" button that dims the interface;
        > system logs only that a private state occurred, not its
        > content.

    -   Facilitators / analysts only see: "Participant in private
        > reflection; please reduce probing."

**5. Simulation / System Architecture (Conceptual)**
----------------------------------------------------

We don't have to implement full quantum hardware initially; we can build
a **classical approximation of Moral Physics** that is structurally
faithful, then hook into quantum systems later (Phase 1--2 of the
Strategic Plan).

### **5.1 Core Data Structures**

-   **Tensor Object:\
    > **

    -   id: prime index p

    -   type: survivor, policy, institution, event

    -   components: semantic vector (defined collaboratively with
        > humans)

    -   rights: permissions, κ, revocation state

    -   links: entanglement edges, weights αₖ

-   **Moral Field State:\
    > **

    -   Λm(t): scalar or field over subdomains

    -   Ξ(t): operator history

    -   Σi(t): moral tension signals

-   **Audit Log:\
    > **

    -   Sequence of operations with a computed "dignity conservation"
        > flag and deltas.

### **5.2 Engine**

-   **Update step (discrete approximation of Ξ(t+1) = f(Ξ(t),
    > Ψᵖ\_moral)):\
    > **

    -   For each timestep or event:

        -   Apply active policies to affected tensors.

        -   Compute deltas in dignity current and Λm (using simplified
            > metrics).

        -   Update entanglement structures (e.g., similarity + shared
            > institutional pathways).

-   **Λm Evolution:\
    > **

    -   Implement a simplified discrete version of the Λm update
        > equation from the monograph:

        -   Λm(t+1) = Λm(t) + Σ δi \* \|\|Ψi\|\|

    -   Visualize Λm as a time series and color-coded on the canvas.

-   **Dignity Conservation Check:\
    > **

    -   Define a practical test:\
        > "Did this operation reduce any person's minimum dignity score
        > below their defined floor?"

    -   If yes → mark as "dignity collapse event".

-   **SMU / Scenario Engine:\
    > **

    -   A scenario = set of tensors + policies + runtime parameters.

    -   Run with different seeds; aggregate results into dashboards for
        > policymakers.

**6. Implementation Roadmap (From Prototype to Reality)**
---------------------------------------------------------

This can align with the phased plan in the Strategic document, but
focused on UX & simulation.

### **Phase A -- Concept & Paper Simulation**

-   Activities:

    -   Storyboards for Survivor, Policymaker, Researcher journeys.

    -   Paper/card simulations of:

        -   PIRTM indexing

        -   Λm changing as you add/remove corrections

        -   QME as string-and-pin boards.

-   Output:

    -   Validated interaction metaphors.

    -   Concrete "simulation rules" that feel intuitive to non-technical
        > participants.

### **Phase B -- Web-Based Prototype (Classical Engine)**

-   Tech: simple web app (React/Vue/etc.) + backend service to run the
    > simulation engine.

-   Features:

    -   Synthetic data only.

    -   SMU playground with:

        -   Hypergraph visualization

        -   Λm gauge

        -   Simple policy operators

    -   Sovereignty-First--inspired consent widget even for test data,
        > to bake the pattern in.

### **Phase C -- Survivor-Centered Pilot (Still Classical)**

-   Small cohort, deep support.

-   Implement:

    -   Real Sovereignty-First workflows (dynamic consent, revocation,
        > κ=0).

    -   Integrity-preserving memory semantics (append-only logs,
        > versioning, no silent deletion).

-   Safeguards:

    -   Ethics review board using the Governance & Audit dashboard to
        > monitor the system's behavior in real time.

### **Phase D -- Institutional Partner Sandbox**

-   Bring in one or two institutions (clinic, legal aid org, social
    > services).

-   Use their **policy** and **process** structure, but start with
    > synthetic or anonymized cases.

-   Run:

    -   Before/after policy simulations.

    -   "Counterfactual" experiments ("What if this rule had been in
        > place for the last 5 years?").

### **Phase E -- Quantum-Linked Experiments (Optional, Later)**

-   Hook the UX to actual quantum simulators as described in Phase 1 of
    > the strategic plan (Tracey's tensor as an entangled moral memory
    > demonstration).

-   From the UX perspective, this might just be:

    -   A toggle between "classical mode" and "quantum-backed mode".

    -   Additional latency, but richer fidelity visualizations for QME.

**7. Example Walkthroughs**
---------------------------

### **7.1 Survivor Flow: "Tracey's Tensor" (Narrative Mode)**

1.  Tracey logs in, sees the Sovereignty-First welcome:

    -   "You are the expert on your experience; you control what happens
        > here."

2.  She sets her consent dials:

    -   Cognitive exploration: medium

    -   Emotional depth: low

    -   Sharing with institutions: off for now

3.  She narrates her experience; the system returns a **tensor card**:

    -   Prime index p

    -   Two or three semantic axes she chose (e.g., "medical neglect",
        > "procedural gaslighting").

4.  On the canvas, she sees:

    -   Her node appear

    -   Several faint possible entanglement suggestions (other similar
        > cases, anonymized).

5.  She clicks "κ = 0":

    -   The UI visually decouples her node from consensus operations;
        > system explains:\
        > "Your experience will never be averaged into group statistics
        > unless you turn this back on."

6.  She is shown how her tensor contributes to Λm if and when she
    > consents to institutional simulations.

### **7.2 Policy Flow: "Test a New Appeals Procedure"**

1.  A policy analyst chooses SMU: "Benefits Appeals System".

2.  They draft a new statute in the Policy Operator Panel (or pick from
    > a library).

3.  They run the simulation for 10 years of synthetic cases:

    -   Λm trend is displayed.

    -   Dignity conservation violations are highlighted.

    -   Entanglement clusters reveal specific demographics
        > disproportionately harmed.

4.  They tweak parameters (response time, default outcomes, escalation
    > paths).

5.  The system shows:

    -   Λm stabilizing.

    -   Fewer dignity collapses.

    -   More "spontaneous ethical restoration" events where previously
        > harmed tensor clusters are remedied.

**8. Guardrails & Open Design Questions**
-----------------------------------------

-   **Privacy vs. Entanglement:\
    > ** How to show QME-like connections without deanonymizing or
    > over-exposing participants?

-   **Legibility:\
    > ** How much math to surface, and when? Use progressive disclosure:

    -   High-level story → intuitive visuals → optional formal view.

-   **Abuse Prevention:\
    > ** Hard constraints in UI preventing:

    -   Running simulations that ignore revocation.

    -   Overriding sovereignty dials.

    -   Deploying policy changes from the simulator without explicit
        > ethical review.
