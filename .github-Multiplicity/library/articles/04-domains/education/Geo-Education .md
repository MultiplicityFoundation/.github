🌱 Multiplicity-Based Geometry Teaching Plan
1. Embrace Multiplicity Theory

Multiplicity Theory encourages holistic, interdisciplinary learning by recognizing the
interconnectedness of knowledge domains and learning styles. In geometry, this means
combining spatial reasoning with real-world applications, storytelling, ethics, art, and
technology.

Dear Teachers
Education, in the world of multiplicity, is not a staircase—it is a garden. It grows in all directions,
at different rhythms, with different forms of flowering. It is no longer a system designed to sort
people, but to support them. Education does not begin or end at any age—it is continuous,
living, and shared. As we move closer to reaching this goal, this lesson was prepared using
educational standards that support the educational standards of grades 8th-10th.

🔺 Differentiated Strategies for Teaching Geometry
A. Visual & Kinesthetic Learners

        ●​ Tangible Models: Use building blocks, 3D printed shapes, or recycled
           materials to physically construct geometric forms.
        ●​ Gardening Geometry: Design raised beds or planting patterns using shapes
           (e.g., hexagonal garden plots) to explore area, perimeter, and tessellation.
        ●​ Movement-Based Learning: Use body-based geometry games where
           students form shapes with string or their arms.

B. Auditory Learners

        ●​ Geometry Story Circles: Tell stories that incorporate geometric concepts (e.g.,
           the “journey of a triangle”), using metaphors and analogies aligned with
           Multiplicity Theory.
        ●​ Collaborative Dialogue: Small-group math talks encourage discussion about
           different ways to solve geometry problems, building empathy and reinforcing
           concepts through social learning.

C. Logical-Mathematical Learners
      ●​ Systems Thinking: Link geometry to larger systems (e.g., urban planning or
         environmental design). Students might redesign their schoolyard using
         geometric optimization.
      ●​ Problem Challenges: Integrate logic puzzles or building design problems that
         require calculating volume or angles.

D. Artistic & Creative Learners

      ●​ Geometry Through Art: Explore mandalas, Islamic tiling, or fractals. Have
         students design geometric art with symmetry and transformation rules.
      ●​ Storyboarding: Use comic strips or animation tools to visualize and narrate
         geometric transformations like reflection or rotation.

💡 Inclusive Technologies
Use PhET simulations, as mentioned in the document, or GeoGebra, which are
accessible, interactive, and allow students to manipulate shapes and explore geometric
concepts in an intuitive way. These are especially beneficial for:

      ●​ Neurodivergent learners
      ●​ Students with physical or visual limitations
      ●​ Learners needing self-paced instruction


🧭 Assessment Strategies (Multiplicity-Aligned)
        ●​ Project Portfolios: A collection of sketches, models, photos, and journal
           entries showing growth in understanding.
        ●​ Peer Evaluation: Students give constructive feedback on each other’s
           geometric models.
        ●​ Empathy Circles: Reflect on how geometry can help design inclusive public
           spaces.

🌆 Project Description: Build a Mini Green City
Step 1: City Planning Challenge
Introduce students to the idea of designing a “green city block” that includes:

       ●​ Housing units (rectangular prisms)
       ●​ Parks (triangles, circles, irregular polygons)
       ●​ Water features (cylinders or cones)
       ●​ Roads or paths (parallel lines, angles, transformations)

🔄 Include a sustainability twist: Each space must maximize green space while
minimizing waste and using renewable energy features.



Step 2: Geometry Integration

Students apply:

       ●​ Area & Perimeter (Calculate total green space)
       ●​ Volume (Design water tanks or housing units)
       ●​ Transformations (Design symmetrical park layouts using rotation/reflection)
       ●​ Pythagorean Theorem (Calculate path lengths or diagonals across city
          blocks)

🧮 Students document each calculation in a group “City Planning Log.”
Step 3: Present & Reflect

       ●​ Students present their models and explain how geometry informed their
          design decisions.
       ●​ Reflect in “Empathy Circles”:
             ○​ How did our designs make the city better for everyone?
             ○​ What would you do differently next time?

Green City Geometry 8th Adhd

Lesson Plan: Designing a Green City - Geometry in Action

Theme: Preparing for the Future ​
Subject Focus: Geometry, Urban Design, Sustainability ​
Grade Level: 8th Grade ​
Duration: 2–3 class periods (modular, adjustable)



Core Learning Objectives
      ●​ Apply geometric concepts including area, perimeter, volume, and
         transformations.
      ●​ Collaborate effectively in groups, developing communication and planning
         skills.
      ●​ Use critical and ethical reasoning to design inclusive, sustainable urban
         spaces.


ADHD-Inclusive Supports

      ●​ Movement-Friendly: Hands-on materials, optional standing workstations,
         movement breaks.
      ●​ Executive Function Scaffolding: Visual schedule, task checklists, role
         assignment.
      ●​ Choice & Autonomy: Students select design focus (e.g., parks, housing,
         transportation).
      ●​ Visual & Kinesthetic Tools: Use of physical models, drawing templates, and
         digital platforms.


Materials

      ●​ Cardboard, paper, scissors, rulers, compasses, markers
      ●​ LEGOs, modeling clay, or recycled materials (optional)
      ●​ Laptops/tablets with GeoGebra or Tinkercad (optional)
      ●​ Role cards, task checklists, timers


Lesson Outline

Day 1: Introduction & Planning

      1.​ Hook Activity (10 min)
             a.​ Present challenge: "Your city needs redesigning to be more
                 sustainable, inclusive, and green. How can geometry help?"
             b.​ Show examples of green city features (rooftop gardens, bike paths,
                 solar panels).
      2.​ Form Teams (5 min)
             a.​ Assign or allow choice. Distribute role cards:
                      i.​ Designer
                      ii.​ Measurer/Calculator
                      iii.​ Planner/Scheduler
                      iv.​ Presenter
      3.​ Plan City Block (30 min)
             a.​ Sketch layout with labeled zones: residential, green space, water
                 feature, public space.
             b.​ Start calculating rough dimensions using area and perimeter formulas.
             c.​ Record in "City Planning Log."


Day 2: Build & Calculate

      1.​ Work Time (45 min)
            a.​ Construct physical or digital model.
            b.​ Apply geometry:
                     i.​ Area/perimeter of parks, roads
                     ii.​ Volume of buildings and water features
                     iii.​ Use transformations for symmetry or repeated patterns
                     iv.​ Pythagorean Theorem for diagonal paths
      2.​ Check-in & Reflect (15 min)
            a.​ Use timers and checklists to assess group progress.
            b.​ Optional movement/stretch break every 20 minutes.


Day 3: Present & Reflect

      1.​ Final Touches (15 min)
             a.​ Finalize models and calculations.
             b.​ Prepare 2-minute group presentation.
      2.​ Group Presentations (30 min)
             a.​ Share designs and explain geometric and ethical reasoning.
             b.​ Option for gallery walk if groups rotate.
      3.​ Empathy Circles (15 min)
             a.​ Reflective questions:
                      i.​ How did our design help different types of people?
                      ii.​ What was hardest about working as a team?
                      iii.​ What would you improve in your next design?
