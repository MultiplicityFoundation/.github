## What Is Multiplicity?

Multiplicity has existed in mathematics, metaphysics, and physics — but until Citizen Gardens, **never in the context of people**.

In set theory, duplicates are erased: the set `{a, a, b}` collapses to `{a, b}`. The repetition is lost. This is the danger society faces too — without active recognition, unique contributions are homogenized, forgotten, flattened.

Citizen Gardens resists this. We treat society as a **multiset** (a *pairing community*), where every person, every act, every voice is counted — not as a data point, but as a **frequency**.

**Multiplicity in Social Physics** is calculated by:

```
M = 2R + 1
```

Where:
- **M** = Multiplicity (the measure of reciprocal energy in a system)
- **R** = Reciprocity (the rate, volume, and momentum of mutual exchange)

| Member State | R Value | Multiplicity (M) | Term |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unpaired (in a set) | 0 | 1 | Singlet |
| Single paired member (in a multiset) | 0.5 | 2 | Doublet |
| Two paired members (in a multiset) | 1 | 3 | Triplet |

> A single individual can only contribute **half** of the energy necessary for value creation — it takes at least two to multiply.

## Counting the Energy of People

> *"Every socio-economic system throughout history has grappled with a fundamental flaw — the miscounting of human energy."*

Traditional systems count by **addition and accumulation**. Multiplicity Theory reveals that social energy is fundamentally **multiplicative**, not additive. When two people interact in reciprocity, the resulting value exceeds the sum — it compounds.

This framework is grounded in the work of **Friedrich Hund**, the German atomic physicist whose three rules of maximum multiplicity (1925) describe how electrons fill orbitals to achieve maximum stability. Citizen Gardens transposes these rules into social physics:

### Hund's Rules — Social Physics Translation

1. **Maximum Multiplicity = Lowest Energy**: A community in maximum reciprocity operates at its most stable and efficient state. Individual burden is dispersed across the network, requiring less energy to be productive.

2. **Single Filing Before Pairing**: Members occupy roles individually before doubling up — ensuring maximum coverage and diversity of contribution before redundancy.

3. **Momentum and Direction Matter**: In early-stage communities (less than half capacity), focused direction yields stability. As communities approach full capacity, broader distributed momentum becomes more beneficial.

### The Fourth Rule (Van Gelder)

> Once a multiset is established, it creates an internal value **twice** the value of anything outside it — making it 50% more logical and economical to invest in the model than to continue without it. At global scale, universal membership yields a **threefold** increase in value over prior external arrangements.

### Novel Mathematical Constructs

- **Λm (Universal Multiplicity Constant)**: A scalar representing the baseline potential for reciprocal interaction within any given social system
- **Ξ(t) (Recursive Operator)**: A temporal operator governing how interactions compound or decay over time — enabling recursive computation of influence, trust, and collective evolution

## The Socio-Atomic Model

The Socio-Atomic Model transposes the mathematical structure of quantum mechanics onto social systems — not as metaphor, but as **method**.

| Particle | Social Analog | Reciprocal Charge | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Proton** | Active contributor / paired member | R ≥ 0.5 | Provides the central force; directly engages in collaborative work within the social nucleus |
| **Neutron** | Infrastructure (buildings, tools, platforms) | Neutral | No inherent charge but gains value through proximity to active participants |
| **Electron** | Peripheral participant / unpaired member | R → 0 | Engagement varies with angle and momentum relative to the nucleus |

### Key Properties

- **Social Bonding** follows a Coulomb-like law — attraction (mutual benefit) increases with reciprocity and decreases with social distance
- **Energy Exchange** between participants is quantized in units of reciprocal engagement: `E = Λm · ΔR`
- **Stability** is achieved when reciprocal forces balance across all participants
- **Multiplicative Emergence**: When two individuals interact, combined multiplicity exceeds the sum: `M_combined = m₁ + m₂ + 2m₁m₂`

## Economy League Multiplicity (ELM)

ELM is the guiding economic and ethical framework of Citizen Gardens. It redefines **wealth as well-being** — not financial accumulation.

### Core Principles

- **Reciprocity is the currency**: Value arises from interaction, not extraction
- **Non-monetary value systems**: Wealth is measured in time, attention, creativity, and care
- **Stewardship over ownership**: Members are agents *and* guardians of a shared future
- **Cultural competence**: Diversity is essential — differences are vital nutrients in the soil of collective well-being

### Atomic-Social Modeling

ELM conceptualizes organizations as socio-atomic structures:
- **Neutrons** → Physical assets (buildings, tools, land)
- **Protons** → Individuals who activate and utilize those assets
- **Electrons** → Community members who engage peripherally, shaped by their reciprocal relationship with the organizational core

## Credit Ecosystem

Citizen Gardens operates a diverse credit system reflecting and rewarding engagement, contribution, and reciprocity.

| Credit Type | Earned By | Frequency | Exchangeable | Transferable |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Ingenuity Awards** | Creative solutions and innovation | As awarded | ✅ | ✅ |
| **Designation Rewards** | Designating property/IP for community use | Monthly | ✅ | ✅ |
| **Opportunity Rewards** | Volunteering, project management, governance roles | Weekly | ✅ | ✅ |
| **Sharing Rewards** | Referring new members | Annually | ❌ | ❌ |
| **Sponsorship Credits** | Monetary contributions | As received | ✅ | ❌ |
| **Intrinsic Credits** | Consistent participation, upholding Code of Conduct, service | Ongoing | ❌ | ❌ |

> **Intrinsic Credits** are the trust metric. They gate eligibility for leadership roles, advanced programs, and community recognition. They cannot be bought or traded — only earned through sustained reciprocity.

### Staking for Good — The Greenhouse Model

Members can stake credits or assets in community projects. Instead of financial interest, they receive **living dividends**: produce, services, community access, or renewed contracts.

```
Example: A member stakes 2,000 credits in an 18-month contract.
The community builds a greenhouse.
After 6 months of setup → 12 months of monthly returns
in vegetables, community credits, or both.
```

## Governance

- **Local Councils as Civic Sentience**: Neighbors diverse in age, skill, and vision — stewards, not rulers

- **Global Resonance Through Local Coherence**: Each node is autonomous but interconnected — a planetary lattice of distributed intelligence
