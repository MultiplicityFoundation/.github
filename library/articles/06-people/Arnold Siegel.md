Arnold W. Siegel (1932--2017) was a forensic engineer based in Los
Angeles who made significant contributions to accident reconstruction,
particularly in the investigation of automobile collisions. After
serving in the U.S. Army during the Korean War, he pursued a career in
engineering, specializing in crash analysis and transportation safety.
Siegel worked extensively as a consultant and expert witness, helping to
analyze motor vehicle accidents, identify causes, and reconstruct crash
dynamics. His expertise made him a key figure in automotive safety,
particularly in the legal and forensic fields.

One of his major professional roles included designing early
anthropometric dummies for crash tests, which are vital for
understanding injury mechanisms in vehicle accidents. His pioneering
work in crash injury biomechanics and transportation safety contributed
to developing safer vehicles and advancing forensic methods for accident
analysis. His legacy includes his long-term impact on vehicle crash
testing and injury prevention.

investigation practices, and his influence continues through awards such

Arnold Siegel\'s observation that \"moving at 58 mph is about 90 feet
per second\" is a practical application of converting speed units and

### **Conversion of Speed from Miles per Hour to Feet per Second:**

1.  **58 miles per hour (mph)** is the speed given, but we often round
    > to **60 mph** for simplicity.

2.  **1 mile = 5280 feet**, and **1 hour = 3600 seconds**.

To convert from mph to feet per second (fps), the formula is:

Speed in fps=Speed in mph×5280 feet3600 seconds

So, at **60 mph**:

Speed in fps=60×52803600=60×1.467=88 feet per second

At **58 mph**:

Speed in fps=58×52803600=58×1.467=85 feet per second

Siegel rounded this value to **90 feet per second** for ease of mental
calculation, which aligns closely with the actual value.

### **Application in Domains:**

    > impact).
