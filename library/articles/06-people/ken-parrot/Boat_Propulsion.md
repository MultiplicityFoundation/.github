                                     Abstract
          This paper presents a propulsion concept where a conventional ma-
      rine propeller is preceded by a vortex-conditioning tube—combining pre-
      swirl stators, post-swirl recovery, and controlled boundary-layer manage-
      ment—to achieve greater thrust and efficiency with reduced turbulence
      and cavitation. Underpinned by proven ducted-propeller theory and en-
      hanced through fractal flow logic (“Flowing with Nature”), this hybrid
      system aligns with the Trifecta principle: Dump → Soak → Clamp.


2     Introduction
Ducted propellers (e.g. Kort nozzles) are known to improve low-speed thrust
and efficiency by reducing tip vortices and increasing mass flow [1]. However,
traditional designs do not incorporate staged swirl management or active flow
conditioning. By embedding vortex-tube geometry, we refine inflow, recover
swirl energy, and shape the wake, mirroring fractal flow harmony.


3     Supporting Physics & Prior Findings
    • Ducted propeller thrust gain at low speed: ∼23.7% increase at 400
      rpm compared to open prop; advantage diminishes at higher rpm due to
      drag and cavitation [2].
    • Pre-swirl stators: Conditioning upstream flow can improve propulsive
      efficiency; experiments show blade pressure fluctuation decreases by ≥10%
      with rounded stator tips, helping to mitigate cavitation [3].
    • Optimized duct geometry: Increasing duct thickness, camber, and
      optimal incidence delivers measurable gains in efficiency [4].

    • Wake-equalizing ducts: Becker-Mewis and similar systems demonstrate
      that straightening or adding contra-swirl to wake flow improves propeller
      efficiency and reduces power usage [5].


4     Concept Design
    1. Pre-swirl stage: Helical fins or stators set a controlled swirl, tuning
       inflow to blade angle.
    2. Rotor: Propeller remains conventional but benefits from aligned inflow
       with reduced incidence.

    3. Post-swirl recovery: Vanes extract leftover swirl and convert it to axial
       thrust.
    4. Outer sleeve (tube-in-tube): Helical micro-ribbed boundary-layer con-
       ditioner; recirculates a small fraction of flow to prevent separation and cool
       e-propulsor electronics.

5     Benefits & Trade-offs

 Mid-speed (acceleration)      Improved response due to cleaner inflow
 Cruise                        Slight drag from tube; efficiency gain ∼5–7% feasible
 Noise & vibration             Tip-vortex suppression, smoother wake; noise cut 3–10 dB


6     Falsifiable Test Path
    • CFD simulations: Baseline vs. vortex-tube intake on hull-form and
      propeller performance; track CT , CP , cavitation index, wake turbulence
      spectra.
    • Cavitation tunnel: Scale model with adjustable pre/post stators; record
      inception threshold and noise spectra with/without tube.
    • Tow-test A/B: Identical hull/propeller systems; one fitted with vortex
      intake, one without; compare fuel use at matched speed/power and acous-
      tic signatures.
