**Dance Me Through the Panic: A Story of Dissonance and Recovery**
==================================================================

### **1. Introduction: The Unraveling**

The year 2022 was the most trying of my life. It began subtly, with the
kind of minor frustrations that feel like cosmic jokes. The dishwasher
would fail mid-cycle. The internet would intermittently stop working.
Life felt like it was constantly throwing curveballs, and these small
domestic failures began to feel deeply unsettling.

This feeling of things being just slightly *off* soon expanded beyond my
home. The parking lot, the supermarket, the busy street outside my
window---everything was beginning to look a little funny from my
perspective. I found myself making connections that I couldn\'t explain,
and a profound sense of confusion set in. My husband, my main source of
support, listened to my ramblings but couldn\'t understand. His loving
skepticism was a painful mirror to my own internal chaos. After one
particularly baffling explanation on my part, he said, with all
seriousness:

\"You're acting like one of those tin-foil-hat people, Tiffany.\"

It's an unfair place to start, as it paints my husband in the worst
light; in truth, he's the hero in this story. But he was right that I
sounded irrational. I couldn\'t articulate the fragmented experiences
that were leading me to these conclusions. I was lost in a swampland
where nothing made sense---not a damned thing. This growing sense of a
world coming undone was the backdrop for a single decision that would
accelerate my crisis and ultimately lead me toward a new understanding.

### **2. The Trigger: An Ode to Cognitive Dissonance**

In October 2022, while participating in an Analytic-Network Coaching
Program, I decided to facilitate a presentation for an international
community. The title was \"Ode to Cognitive Dissonance,\" and in it, I
planned to share my personal history of childhood sexual abuse. My
intention was bold and, in retrospect, reckless: I wanted to invoke
*feelings* of cognitive dissonance in the audience. As I later admitted,
my goal was to \"scramble people\'s brains.\"

I accomplished what I set out to do, but the aftermath was not what I
expected. During a follow-up discussion, an audience member posed a
critical question that cut right through me:

\"is that ethical?\"

The question landed with the weight of an accusation. I felt shaken up
and wildly exposed. As someone who aims to build containers of \"joyous
curiosity,\" the question was a direct hit. I had used my deepest wound
not to connect, but to destabilize---a profound violation of my own
values. That question has stayed with me, carried around as a shame
blanket ever since. My attempt to publicly confront dissonance had only
amplified my own, triggering a private descent I was completely
unprepared for.

### **3. The Descent: When the World Speaks in Riddles**

With my husband away on a short deployment, I was left alone to navigate
the emotional fallout from my presentation. I didn\'t have the proper
supports in place, and what followed was a strange kind of psychosis.
Looking back, I can see this wasn\'t random madness; it was my internal
system, my \"parts,\" screaming for help in the only ways they knew how.
My world, already feeling a little funny, began actively speaking to me
in riddles.

My experiences during this time were a series of bizarre, unsettling
events that fueled a growing paranoia:

-   **A Haunting Warning:** I opened up to a Spiritual Guide, who, after
    > a few hours of conversation, cautioned me to be careful what I
    > said, lest people think I was some kind of \"paranoid
    > schizophrenic.\" I was beside myself. Was there no one who could
    > understand?

-   **A Glitching Reality:** The virtual reality game Beat Saber, once a
    > reliable source of comfort and active meditation, began to
    > constantly malfunction. A controller would stop working, a charged
    > headset would suddenly lose power---each glitch fed my suspicion
    > that I was being watched or manipulated.

-   **Familial Misunderstanding and Comfort:** During a visit with my
    > parents, I turned to my mom for support. Instead, she confirmed my
    > paranoid behavior, saying, \"this isn't like you to act like
    > this.\" Yet in the same visit, we escaped to the beach, a place
    > where we often connect. We walked barefoot in the sand, watched
    > mesmerizing sunsets, and she held me as I cried over my
    > presentation. This mix of misunderstanding and comfort only
    > deepened the tragedy of my isolation.

-   **Perverted Pep Talks:** Toward the end of the visit, a bizarre
    > conversation between my parents about old pet fish became twisted
    > in my mind into \"perverted sex talk.\" I retreated to my room for
    > a frantic internal pep talk: *\"Not everyone is trying to have sex
    > with you, Tiffany....\"*

Isolated and terrified, believing my entire world had turned against me,
I did what so many of us do in moments of desperation: I turned to a
stranger who seemed to offer the one thing I couldn\'t find anywhere
else---understanding.

### **4. The Stand-In Protector: An Alliance in the Abyss**

I connected with a stranger on LinkedIn who, unlike my family or
spiritual guide, didn\'t think I was crazy. He listened, he engaged, and
he seemed to understand the fragmented world I was describing. This
alliance was the work of a firefighter part---a reactive, impulsive
protector that emerges after a crisis to douse emotional flames with any
tool available, regardless of the collateral damage to my marriage or my
integrity. He quickly became a \"stand-in protector,\" filling the void
left by my deployed husband.

This relationship was a lifeline. We laughed and bonded over the absurd
fact that we had both been called a \"paranoid schizophrenic.\" For the
first time in months, I felt a sense of relief and security. However,
the dynamic soon shifted toward perversion. He gave me a running
\"joke\" nickname that felt both validating and deeply inappropriate:

**\"Menopausal Borderline Histrionic Paranoid Schizophrenic.\"**

This firefighter part's desperate attempt to name and validate my
overwhelming pain was a distorted cry for help. I was in a committed
10-year marriage, yet I allowed these increasingly inappropriate
conversations to continue. In my state of panic and isolation, the
feeling of having an ally---of not being delusional---mattered more to
me than anything else. I was clinging to a sense of safety, unaware that
this fraught alliance was pushing me closer to the breaking point.

### **5. The Breaking Point: Hospitalization and a Shattered Reality**

My husband\'s homecoming was not the joyful reunion we both needed.
There was an immediate rift between us, an awkwardness born from my
inability to explain my experiences and his inability to understand
them. When talking failed, I tried to *show* him my psychosis. This was
the complete takeover by my firefighter parts. With my internal system
so dysregulated and my core \"Self\" obscured, these parts acted out in
extreme ways because they had no other strategy. My behaviors were
deeply traumatizing for him, and he pleaded with me to \"STOP IT!\" but
I couldn\'t.

After I completely \"flipped out\" and \"lost my marbles,\" it was clear
this was a predictable system collapse, not a personal failing. My
husband suggested we go to the Emergency Room. There, after the staff
was told I\'d had a \"change of mental status,\" I voluntarily checked
myself into a psychiatric hospital. The hospitalization was a necessary
external intervention to contain an internal war.

Less than 24 hours after my initial release, I was back in the ER.
Another \"change of mental status\" led to a second, back-to-back
hospitalization. My reality had completely shattered, and my marriage
was teetering on the edge of divorce. It was inside the structured walls
of the hospital that I would finally begin to find a framework to
reassemble the pieces.

### **6. Inside the Walls: Finding a New Framework**

The psychiatric hospital wasn\'t like the movies. It was a place of
daily routines, group therapy sessions, and surprisingly normal people
all working through their own issues. I colored, read books, and
discovered a key insight about myself: when I am in a state of
heightened stress, I seek control, and I had been physically holding my
breath for months. During this time, I was formally diagnosed with
Bipolar I Disorder.

After my release, I attended an outpatient Mood Clinic, but the tool
that truly helped me make sense of my terrifying spiral was the
**Internal Family Systems (IFS)** model. IFS proposes that we are all
made up of multiple \"parts\" and a core Self. The diagnosis of Bipolar
I Disorder was the medical language for what my system was doing: the
extreme, polarized behavior of my firefighter parts in the absence of
Self-leadership. My entire crisis could be understood through this new
lens.

#### **The Exile**

This was the vulnerable, 9-year-old part of me that held the trauma of
childhood sexual abuse. Exiles are young parts that have been pushed
away for their own protection. When I shared this part\'s story in my
\"Ode to Cognitive Dissonance\" presentation, I brought my deepest, most
wounded exile onto a public stage without permission from the rest of my
internal system. This was the trigger.

#### **The Protectors (Managers and Firefighters)**

Protectors come in two forms: proactive **Managers** who try to control
life to prevent trauma from being triggered, and reactive
**Firefighters** who emerge after the fact to desperately put out the
emotional fire. My \"Business Tiffany\" part is a manager. It was the
screaming internal voice that warned, \"turn back now!\" before my
presentation. When I did it anyway, my firefighter parts reacted. My
hypersexuality, intense paranoia, social isolation, and desperate
attachment to the \"stand-in protector\" were all firefighter parts
trying to manage the overwhelming pain of the exposed exile. They were
desperate measures with little regard for collateral damage.

#### **The Self**

According to IFS, the Self is our core---the calm, compassionate,
confident, and curious inner leader that is our true essence. The entire
goal of my recovery became about unburdening my parts from their extreme
roles so I could reconnect with this Self and allow it to lead my
internal system once more.

This framework was a revelation. My behaviors weren\'t random madness;
they were the predictable actions of a traumatized inner system in
desperate need of leadership. This understanding didn\'t erase the pain,
but it provided a map for the long, challenging work of recovery.

### **7. Conclusion: The Dance of Recovery**

The journey back has been slow and difficult. My husband---the true hero
in this story---has stood by my side, even as he struggled to understand
why I lied and acted out in such destructive ways. We are rebuilding our
marriage, a new chapter we call \"Tiff & Greg 2.0.\"

The work continues. I am learning that I am my own life\'s problem, that
I\'ve been held back by dormant low self-esteem and a deep difficulty in
loving myself. My aspirations now are focused on creation and
connection: developing my Dysfunctional Moments™ Community, launching my
business EnvisionerZ™, and even bringing a whimsical idea like Brain
Fart in a Jar™ to life.

Just when I think I\'m through the worst of it, life has a way of
pulling me back into the muck. But now, I see it differently. I am
learning to dance with the panic, to find the growth in the struggle.
This dark, ugly chapter of my life has become an opportunity to build
something new from the shattered pieces.

I hope over time, the shit begins to smell increasingly like a bed of
roses. My feeling is, if I'm going to live in the shit, why not attempt
to make the best out of a stinky situation?

It's like the old saying goes: when life gives you lemons, don't tuck
'em, make lemonade!
