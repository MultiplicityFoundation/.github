### **21st Century (2000--2025)**

10. **Terence Tao (b. 1975)**:

    -   Contributed to the study of prime gaps, including collaboration
        > on breakthroughs such as bounded gaps between primes.

    -   Advanced understanding of prime numbers through harmonic
        > analysis and additive number theory.

11. **Yitang Zhang (b. 1955)**:

    -   Proved there are infinitely many pairs of primes separated by a
        > bounded gap (2013), an important step toward resolving the
        > twin prime conjecture.

12. **James Maynard (b. 1987)**:

    -   Made significant progress on the distribution of primes,
        > including improvements on bounded prime gaps and small gaps
        > between primes.

13. **Ben Green (b. 1977)** and **Terence Tao**:

    -   Proved the **Green-Tao theorem**, stating that there are
        > arbitrarily long arithmetic progressions of prime numbers.

14. **Manjul Bhargava (b. 1974)**:

    -   While primarily known for his work in algebra and elliptic
        > curves, his contributions indirectly impact prime number
        > theory through modular forms and L-functions.

15. **Akshay Venkatesh (b. 1981)**:

    -   Worked on automorphic forms and their connections to prime
        > number theory.

16. **Harald Helfgott (b. 1977)**:

    -   Proved the **ternary Goldbach conjecture**, which states that
        > every odd integer greater than 5 is the sum of three primes.

17. **Ken Ono (b. 1968)**:

    -   Studied modular forms and partition theory, providing insights
        > into prime numbers.

### **Mid 20th Century (1950--2000)**

5.  **Paul Erdős (1913--1996)**:

    -   Made extensive contributions to the distribution of prime
        > numbers, including probabilistic methods in number theory.

    -   Worked on the **Erdős-Kac theorem**, which describes the normal
        > distribution of the number of prime factors of integers.

6.  **Enrico Bombieri (b. 1940)**:

    -   Proved the **Bombieri-Vinogradov theorem**, a significant result
        > on the distribution of primes in arithmetic progressions.

    -   Worked on sieve methods and their applications to prime number
        > theory.

7.  **Chen Jingrun (1933--1996)**:

    -   Proved **Chen's theorem**, stating that every sufficiently large
        > even number can be expressed as the sum of a prime and a
        > semiprime (a product of two primes).

8.  **Andrew Wiles (b. 1953)**:

    -   While best known for proving Fermat's Last Theorem, Wiles's
        > methods included deep insights into modular forms and
        > L-functions, foundational for understanding primes.

9.  **Pierre Deligne (b. 1944)**:

    -   Extended the work on the Weil conjectures, with implications for
        > prime number theory, particularly in algebraic geometry.

### **Early 20th Century (1900--1950)**

1.  **G.H. Hardy (1877--1947)**:

    -   Known for his work with **John E. Littlewood** on the
        > Hardy-Littlewood Conjectures, which include conjectures about
        > the distribution of prime numbers.

    -   Co-authored work on the Riemann zeta function and its
        > implications for prime number theory.

2.  **John E. Littlewood (1885--1977)**:

    -   Collaborated with Hardy on prime number distributions and
        > conjectures.

    -   Proved the existence of arbitrarily large gaps between
        > consecutive primes.

3.  **Srinivasa Ramanujan (1887--1920)**:

    -   Contributed to understanding the properties of prime numbers
        > through his work on highly composite numbers and modular
        > forms.

4.  **Atle Selberg (1917--2007)**:

    -   Introduced the **Selberg sieve**, a powerful method in analytic
        > number theory for estimating the density of primes.

    -   Proved results related to the distribution of primes in
        > arithmetic progressions.

### **18. Carl Friedrich Gauss (1777--1855):**

-   **Contributions**:

    -   Introduced the **Prime Number Theorem** conjecture, which
        > estimates the distribution of prime numbers using the
        > logarithmic integral function: π(x)∼xln⁡x\\pi(x) \\sim

    -   Worked extensively on modular arithmetic and quadratic
        > reciprocity, laying foundational principles for later
        > developments in number theory.

### **20. Peter Gustav Lejeune Dirichlet (1805--1859):**

-   **Contributions**:

    -   Proved the **Dirichlet theorem on arithmetic progressions**,
        > stating that an arithmetic progression a+nda + nda+nd contains

### **21. Ernst Eduard Kummer (1810--1893):**

-   **Contributions**:

    -   Developed **ideal numbers** to address problems in number theory
        > related to unique factorization, particularly in cyclotomic
        > fields.

    -   His work on Fermat\'s Last Theorem for regular primes indirectly
        > advanced the study of primes.

### **23. Joseph Liouville (1809--1882):**

-   **Contributions**:

    -   Developed tools for the study of prime numbers, including
        > methods for approximating arithmetic functions.

    -   Worked on the distribution of prime numbers and their

### **24. Leopold Kronecker (1823--1891):**

-   **Contributions**:

    -   Extended the study of prime numbers to algebraic number fields.

    -   Developed foundational ideas in number theory, particularly
        > involving primes in quadratic forms and modular arithmetic.

### **25. Richard Dedekind (1831--1916):**

-   **Contributions**:

    -   Introduced the concept of **Dedekind domains**, extending the
        > idea of unique factorization to algebraic integers.

    -   Studied prime ideals, generalizing the concept of prime numbers
        > to abstract algebra.

### **26. Evariste Galois (1811--1832):**

-   **Contributions**:

    -   While primarily known for founding **Galois theory**, his work
        > indirectly impacted the study of primes by linking them to
        > field extensions and modular arithmetic.

### **27. James Joseph Sylvester (1814--1897):**

-   **Contributions**:

    -   Explored the properties of prime numbers and their relationships
        > to quadratic forms.

    -   Made significant contributions to matrix theory, which later
        > influenced computational approaches to prime analysis.

### **29. Christian Goldbach (1690--1764):**

-   **Contributions**:

    -   Formulated the **Goldbach conjecture**, stating that every even
        > integer greater than 2 can be expressed as the sum of two
        > primes.

    -   Proposed the weaker conjecture that every odd number greater
        > than 5 is the sum of three primes.

### **30. Joseph-Louis Lagrange (1736--1813):**

-   **Contributions**:

    -   Worked on quadratic forms and modular arithmetic, tools that
        > play a critical role in prime number theory.

    -   Proved that every positive integer is the sum of four squares, a
        > result linked to primes via their representations in quadratic
        > forms.

-   **Contributions**:

    -   While primarily known for work in physics, d'Alembert made early
        > contributions to analytic methods for studying series and
        > sequences, tools later applied to the study of prime numbers.

### **33. Fermat's Influence on This Period:**

-   **Fermat's Work**:

    -   Fermat's earlier discoveries about primes (e.g., **Fermat\'s
        > Little Theorem**) greatly influenced mathematicians of the
        > 18th century, including Euler and Lagrange.

    -   Investigations into Fermat primes (22n+12\^{2\^n} + 122n+1)
        > inspired new research into the nature of primes.

### **34. Pierre de Fermat (1607--1665):**

-   **Contributions**:

    -   Introduced **Fermat\'s Little Theorem**, which states that for
        > any integer aaa and prime ppp: ap−1≡1(modp)a\^{p-1} \\equiv 1
        > \\pmod{p}ap−1≡1(modp)

    -   Studied **Fermat primes**, primes of the form 22n+12\^{2\^n} +
        > 122n+1, conjecturing (incorrectly) that all such numbers are
        > prime.

    -   Proposed the **Fermat conjecture** (part of modern modular
        > arithmetic and number theory).

### **35. Marin Mersenne (1588--1648):**

-   **Contributions**:

    -   Studied **Mersenne primes**, primes of the form 2p−12\^p -
        > 12p−1, where ppp itself is a prime number.

    -   Created a network of correspondence with other mathematicians,
        > popularizing prime number studies.

### **36. John Pell (1611--1685):**

-   **Contributions**:

    -   Investigated number theory problems involving modular arithmetic

### **37. Johann Heinrich Rahn (1622--1676):**

### **38. Christiaan Huygens (1629--1695):**

-   **Contributions**:

    -   While primarily known for his work in physics and astronomy,

### **39. Frans van Schooten (1615--1660):**

### **40. Niccolò Fontana Tartaglia (1499--1557):**

### **41. Pedro Nunes (1502--1578):**

### **42. Michael Stifel (1487--1567):**

    -   Worked on numerical sequences and early forms of logarithms,

    -   Discussed arithmetic progressions and their connection to
        > divisors, a precursor to later prime studies.

### **43. Adam Ries (1492--1559):**

-   **Contributions**:

    -   Known for his practical arithmetic work, which influenced early
        > explorations into factorization and properties of integers,
        > including primes.

### **44. Robert Recorde (1512--1558):**

-   **Contributions**:

    -   Introduced new methods for performing arithmetic calculations,
        > including factorization techniques that intersected with early
        > prime number studies.

### **45. Girolamo Cardano (1501--1576):**

-   **Contributions**:

    -   Best known for his work in algebra, Cardano also examined
        > properties of integers and divisors, indirectly influencing
        > prime number theory.

### **46. Simon Stevin (1548--1620):**

-   **Contributions**:

    -   Advanced arithmetic and decimal systems, providing tools that
        > supported the study of divisors and modular arithmetic,
        > essential for prime analysis.

### **47. Francois Viète (1540--1603):**

-   **Contributions**:

    -   Known as the \"father of modern algebra,\" Viète worked on
        > numerical equations and systems that indirectly contributed to
        > prime number studies.

### **48. Levi ben Gerson (Gersonides, 1288--1344)**:

-   A Jewish mathematician, philosopher, and astronomer from Provence,
    > Gersonides worked on combinatorics and the foundations of number
    > theory. While his work did not directly focus on prime numbers,
    > his explorations in arithmetic and proportions were foundational.

### **49. Nicole Oresme (1323--1382)**:

-   A French philosopher, mathematician, and physicist, Oresme explored
    > the properties of numbers, particularly in relation to infinite
    > series and geometric progressions. Though not directly related to
    > primes, his work laid groundwork for numerical analysis.

### **50. Jean Buridan (ca. 1300--1358)**:

-   While primarily known for his contributions to philosophy and
    > mechanics, Buridan also engaged in mathematical inquiries that
    > touched on the understanding of numerical properties.

### **51. Leonardo of Pisa (Fibonacci, ca. 1170--1250)**

-   Fibonacci introduced the **Hindu-Arabic numeral system** to Europe
    > through his seminal work, *Liber Abaci* (1202). While *Liber
    > Abaci* is best known for the Fibonacci sequence, it also included
    > problems and solutions related to factors, divisors, and the
    > properties of numbers, which are foundational to prime number
    > studies.

### **52. Nasir al-Din al-Tusi (1201--1274)**

-   A Persian polymath, al-Tusi made significant contributions to
    > mathematics, including works on arithmetic and number theory.
    > While not directly focused on primes, his refinements in
    > mathematical methods influenced the study of numerical properties.

### **53. Jordanus de Nemore (fl. 13th century)**

-   Jordanus was a medieval mathematician who wrote on arithmetic and
    > algebra. His treatises often explored the properties of numbers,
    > including ratios and proportions, laying groundwork for systematic
    > number theory.

### **54. Ibn al-Banna (1256--1321)**

-   An Andalusian mathematician, Ibn al-Banna wrote extensively on
    > arithmetic and algebra. His works included methods for handling
    > numbers and their relationships, contributing indirectly to the
    > study of primes.

### **55. Bhāskara II (1114--1185)**

-   An Indian mathematician and astronomer, Bhāskara II is renowned for
    > his work in *Lilavati* and *Bijaganita*. While not directly
    > focused on primes, he developed advanced techniques in arithmetic
    > and algebra, including modular arithmetic, which is fundamental to
    > modern prime number studies.

### **56. Omar Khayyam (1048--1131) *(slightly earlier, but influential in this period)***

-   A Persian polymath known for his work in algebra, Khayyam made
    > contributions to understanding the properties of numbers. Though
    > his work was not specific to primes, his advancements influenced
    > later developments in number theory.

### **57. Al-Samaw\'al (ca. 1130--1180)**

-   A Jewish mathematician active in the Islamic world, Al-Samaw\'al
    > contributed to algebra and arithmetic, including the systematic
    > handling of polynomials and integers. His work indirectly
    > supported later explorations in prime numbers.

### **58. Al-Khazini (fl. 12th century)**

-   A mathematician and astronomer of Persian origin, Al-Khazini worked
    > on problems involving numerical calculations and ratios. While his
    > focus was more on applications, his mathematical rigor supported
    > the broader study of number properties.

### **59. John of Seville (12th century)**

-   A translator and mathematician in Christian Spain, John of Seville
    > helped introduce Arabic mathematical texts to European scholars.
    > These included works on arithmetic and algebra, which contained
    > foundational methods for handling numbers.

### **60. Al-Biruni (973--1048)**

-   A Persian polymath, Al-Biruni worked extensively on mathematics and
    > astronomy. His studies of numbers included modular arithmetic and
    > arithmetic properties, which are important to understanding
    > primes.

### **61. Ibn al-Haytham (Alhazen, 965--1040)**

-   A prominent Arab mathematician and physicist, Ibn al-Haytham
    > contributed to number theory in his work on perfect numbers and
    > their connection to primes. His analysis of integer properties
    > indirectly influenced the study of primes.

### **62. Al-Karaji (ca. 953--1029)**

-   An Iranian mathematician, Al-Karaji made significant contributions
    > to algebra and arithmetic. He introduced systematic methods for
    > handling polynomials and integers, including early ideas that
    > relate to divisors and primality.

### **63. Bhāskara I (600--680, earlier but influential)**

-   Although Bhāskara I predates this period, his works on arithmetic
    > and modular arithmetic were studied and expanded during this time.
    > These ideas were indirectly linked to prime number research.

### **64. Chinese Mathematicians**

-   During this period, Chinese mathematicians worked on problems
    > involving remainders and modular arithmetic, such as those related
    > to the Chinese Remainder Theorem. While not explicitly about
    > primes, these studies were fundamental to understanding number
    > theory.

### **65. Diophantus of Alexandria (ca. 200--284 CE)**

-   Known as the \"father of algebra,\" Diophantus studied integer
    > solutions to equations (*Diophantine equations*), which intersect
    > with prime number theory. His work influenced later mathematicians
    > in understanding the properties of numbers.

### **66. Aryabhata (476--550 CE)**

-   An Indian mathematician and astronomer, Aryabhata worked on modular
    > arithmetic, divisors, and remainders, which are fundamental to
    > understanding primality and number theory.

### **67. Brahmagupta (598--668 CE)**

-   Another Indian mathematician, Brahmagupta expanded on Aryabhata's
    > work. He studied integer properties and methods for solving
    > equations, laying a foundation for understanding primes and their
    > roles in number systems.

### **68. Al-Khwarizmi (ca. 780--850 CE)**

-   A Persian mathematician, Al-Khwarizmi wrote extensively on
    > arithmetic and algebra. His algorithms for calculations influenced
    > later work in number theory, including the study of primes.

### **69. Al-Kindi (801--873 CE)**

-   Known as the \"Philosopher of the Arabs,\" Al-Kindi applied
    > mathematics to cryptography. While not directly studying primes,
    > his cryptographic work relied on properties of numbers, including
    > their divisibility and primality.

### **70. Al-Uqlidisi (920--980 CE)**

-   Al-Uqlidisi worked on decimal arithmetic and number systems. His
    > studies of numbers included early discussions on divisors and
    > properties related to primes.

### **71. Thābit ibn Qurra (826--901 CE)**

-   A prominent Arab mathematician, Thābit made contributions to number
    > theory, including studies on amicable numbers, which are connected
    > to primes through their definitions and relationships.

### **72. Alhazen (Ibn al-Haytham, 965--1040 CE)**

-   Known for his work on optics and mathematics, Alhazen studied
    > perfect numbers, which are closely linked to prime numbers via the
    > Euclidean formula.

### **73. Pythagoras (ca. 570--495 BCE)**

-   The Pythagorean school recognized the significance of numbers,
    > including the distinction between prime and composite numbers.
    > They were among the first to explore properties of primes
    > systematically.

### **74. Euclid (ca. 300 BCE)**

-   Euclid\'s *Elements* contains the first known formal study of prime
    > numbers. Key contributions include:

    -   The proof that there are infinitely many primes (Book IX,
        > Proposition 20).

    -   The fundamental theorem of arithmetic, which states that every
        > integer greater than 1 is either a prime or a product of
        > primes in a unique way (Book VII).

### **75. Eratosthenes (ca. 276--194 BCE)**

-   Known for devising the *Sieve of Eratosthenes*, an efficient
    > algorithm to find all prime numbers up to a given limit. This
    > method is still taught and used in introductory number theory.

### **76. Archimedes (ca. 287--212 BCE)**

-   Though not directly focused on primes, Archimedes developed
    > numerical methods and algorithms that were foundational for later
    > studies in number theory.

### **77. Apollonius of Perga (ca. 262--190 BCE)**

-   Primarily known for his work in geometry, Apollonius indirectly
    > influenced number theory by advancing mathematical rigor and
    > problem-solving techniques.

### **78. Nicomachus of Gerasa (ca. 60--120 CE) *(slightly post-1 CE)***

-   In his *Introduction to Arithmetic*, Nicomachus classified numbers
    > and discussed prime numbers in a systematic way, influencing later
    > Neo-Pythagorean thought.

### **79. Indian and Babylonian Mathematicians (ca. 2000 BCE--1 CE)**

-   Early Indian mathematicians, as seen in the *Sulba Sutras*, and
    > Babylonian scribes, explored properties of numbers for practical
    > applications like construction and astronomy. Though their focus
    > wasn\'t explicitly on primes, they laid the groundwork for
    > numerical analysis.
