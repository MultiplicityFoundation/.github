The calculus acts on configurations (Q, p), where Q is an integral symmetric matrix and p is an integral
vector (a characteristic/Pontryagin-refinement datum). A deterministic rewrite strategy produces a
canonical normal form NF(Q, p) that is invariant under the generated equivalences.




1. Configurations and semantics

1.1 Configuration space

A configuration is a pair


$$ (Q,p),\qquad Q\in\mathrm{Sym}_n(\mathbb Z),\ p\in\mathbb Z^n. $$


Interpretation: Q is a lattice pairing (e.g., an intersection form on a chosen basis), and p is extra structure
(e.g., the spin characteristic element / Pontryagin refinement in that basis).


1.2 Congruence (“gauge”) action

A unimodular basis change U ∈ GLn (Z) acts by


$$ (Q,p)\mapsto (U^T Q U,\ U^{-1}p). $$


Slides are elementary generators for this action.

3. Rewriting logic: explicit rules on configurations
We write rewrite rules as


$$ \langle Q,p\rangle \;\Rightarrow\; \langle Q',p'\rangle $$


possibly under an admissibility predicate.


3.1 Slide rule (always admissible)

(SLIDE)


$$ \frac{}{\langle Q,p\rangle\Rightarrow\langle U^TQU,\ U^{-1}p\rangle} $$


where U = Uij (ε) = I + εEij .


3.2 Blow-up rule (always admissible)

(BLOWUP)


$$   \frac{}{\langle        Q,p\rangle\Rightarrow\langle       Q\oplus[\sigma],\   p\oplus   0\rangle}   \qquad
\sigma\in{\pm1}. $$


We formalize blow-down as: find a unimodular U such that


$$ U^T Q U = Q'\oplus[\sigma]. $$

4. Deterministic rewrite-to-normal-form strategy
The central idea is: words are not normalized syntactically; states are normalized deterministically.


Define a deterministic normalization map


$$ \mathsf N:\ (Q,p)\mapsto\mathrm{NF}(Q,p). $$




                                                        3
We obtain a normalized step relation:


$$ (Q,p)\xRightarrow{g} \mathsf N\big(g(Q,p)\big) $$


for any generator g admissible at (Q, p).


4.1 Canonical witness selection for partial reductions

To make N deterministic, every time a partial rule (BLOWDOWN/HYPCANCEL) is applicable, we choose the
witness by a fixed minimization procedure.


Canonical blow-down choice

Among all primitive v that split off a [σ], select the one minimizing the tuple


$$ (|v|_2,\ \mathrm{lex}(v)). $$


Then among unimodular         U with U en = v and U T QU = Q′ ⊕ [σ], choose the U minimizing a
deterministic tie-break key (e.g., lexicographic order of the last row, then of the full matrix entries).


Canonical hyperbolic cancellation choice

Among all primitive hyperbolic pairs (u, v) that split off H2 , select the one minimizing


$$ (\max(|u|_2,|v|_2),\ \mathrm{lex}(u),\ \mathrm{lex}(v)). $$


Then choose a canonical completing U as above.


       Practical note: in plumbing testbeds, a bounded search over short primitive vectors/pairs
       typically finds these quickly.


Stage N1 — Stabilization reduction


     1. While a canonical blow-down is admissible, apply (BLOWDOWN) using the canonical witness.
     2. While a canonical hyperbolic cancellation is admissible, apply (HYPCANCEL) using the canonical
        witness.
     3. Repeat until neither applies.

This strictly decreases rank whenever it fires, so it terminates.


Stage N2 — Global congruence normalization (real-place basis fix) Apply a deterministic lattice
reduction procedure to fix the remaining congruence freedom, e.g.


      • compute an LLL-reduced basis for the lattice with Gram matrix Q,
      • among all LLL-valid reduced bases, choose the one that lexicographically minimizes the coordinates
        of p,

      • fix a sign convention (first nonzero entry of p positive; then lex-min for remaining symmetries).

This yields a reproducible representative in the congruence class.


Stage N3 — Prime-local block normalization (optional but recommended) For each selected prime ℓ
(and optionally a depth r ):


     1. Compute the ℓ-adic Jordan decomposition of (Q, p) ⊗ Zℓ : $$ (Q,p)\otimes\mathbb
        Z_\ell\cong\bigoplus_{e\ge0} \ell^e(Q_{\ell,e},\ p_{\ell,e}). $$
     2. If using a projector Πℓr , truncate to 0 ≤ e < r .
     3. Sort blocks by a canonical key, e.g. $$ (e,\ \mathrm{rank},\ \det\bmod (\mathbb Z_\ell^\times)^2,\
        \text{Hasse/2-adic refinements},\ \text{coordinates of }p_{\ell,e}). $$

The result is NF(Q, p). If you only need a stable normal form, you can stop at N2; N3 makes the prime-tier
structure explicit.




5. Operator words, evaluation, and equivalence

5.1 Words

A word is a sequence of generator symbols


$$ w=g_m\cdots g_2g_1] subject to admissibility of partial inverses at runtime.


5.2 Normalized evaluation

Define [ \mathrm{EvalNF}(w;Q,p):=\mathsf N\big(\llbracket w\rrbracket(Q,p)\big). $$


Two words are equivalent on input (Q, p) if they yield the same normal form.


5.3 “Resonance = 1” criterion (topological/move invariance)

Replace any scoring notion by strict equality of canonical outputs:


$$ \mathrm{EvalNF}(w_1;Q,p)=\mathrm{EvalNF}(w_2;Q,p). $$


This is the move-invariance condition.

6.1 Plumbing input

A plumbing description consists of:


     • A finite graph Γ = (V , E).
     • For each vertex v ∈ V , an oriented D 4 -bundle over S 4 with structure group SO(4), specified by
       bundle parameters (av , bv ) ∈ Z ⊕ Z (a standard π3 (SO(4)) ≅ Z ⊕ Z coordinate choice).
     • Each edge represents a plumbing along fiber/base disks with a chosen sign convention.

Let n = ∣V ∣ and choose the basis {xv }v∈V ⊂ H 4 (W ; Z) given by the core S 4 of each bundle.


Define Q ∈ Symn (Z) by:


     • Diagonal: $$ Q_{vv} = e_v,\qquad e_v:=a_v+b_v\ \ \text{(Euler number in this convention)}. $$
     • Off-diagonal: for v  w ,=$$ Q_{vw} = \sum_{\text{edges }(v,w)} \epsilon_{vw}, $$ where ϵvw ∈ {±1}
       is the plumbing sign (often all +1 in standard Milnor plumbings).

This is the usual “diagonal = self-intersection, edges = intersection” plumbing matrix pattern, now in degree
4 for an 8-manifold.


In the same basis {xv }, define a vector p ∈ Zn by assigning each component from the vertex bundle
parameters:


$$ (p)_v := a_v-b_v\quad\text{(a standard choice compatible with }p_1\text{ conventions up to overall
factors)}. $$


Spin constraint / admissibility: to be in the spin setting, require the appropriate mod 2 parity condition on
the vertex data (equivalently, the induced w2 (W ) = 0); in many plumbing conventions this forces each
Euler number ev to be even.


       If your convention uses p1 directly, you can set (p)v proportional to the local p1 -coefficient at
       the vertex; the rewrite calculus is unchanged (it only needs an integral vector transforming as
       p ↦ U −1 p).

Once (Q, p) is formed for the coboundary W 8 , define:


     • Signature σ(W ) = sign(Q).
     • Quadratic value (over Q) $$ p_W^2 := p^T Q^{-1} p\in\mathbb Q. $$

Then a boundary invariant (for homotopy 7-spheres in the relevant class) is built from


$$ \mu\equiv \frac{p_W^2-\sigma(W)}{224}\pmod{1} $$


(up to convention choices). This makes the 2- and 7-primary structure explicit via the denominator.

6.5 Admissibility tests in plumbing cases (explicit)

In plumbing-derived Q, admissibility for cancellations often becomes concrete:


Blow-down test (**±1**********************************************-sphere split): search for
primitive v with


$$ v^TQv=\pm1. $$


If found, attempt to complete v to a unimodular basis and check if the last basis vector is orthogonal to the
others (i.e., off-diagonal last row/column can be eliminated by slides).


Hyperbolic split test: search for primitive u, v with


$$ u^TQu=0,\quad v^TQv=0,\quad u^TQv=1, $$


and then verify orthogonal split by completing (u, v) to a unimodular basis and reducing cross-terms via
slides.


These tests are exactly what Stage N1 requires.




7. Compact “rewriting logic” summary

7.1 Rule schema list

      • (SLIDE) ⟨Q, p⟩ ⇒ ⟨U T QU , U −1 p⟩ for U = I ± Eij .
      • (BLOWUP) ⟨Q, p⟩ ⇒ ⟨Q ⊕ [σ], p ⊕ 0⟩.
      • (BLOWDOWN) ⟨Q, p⟩ ⇒ ⟨Q′ , π(U −1 p)⟩ if U T QU = Q′ ⊕ [σ] (canonical witness).
      • (HYPADD) ⟨Q, p⟩ ⇒ ⟨Q ⊕ H2 , p ⊕ (0, 0)⟩.
      • (HYPCANCEL) ⟨Q, p⟩ ⇒ ⟨Q′ , π(U −1 p)⟩ if U T QU = Q′ ⊕ H2 (canonical witness).

7.2 Deterministic strategy

Define NF(Q, p) = N(Q, p) by:


     1. Apply canonical (BLOWDOWN)/(HYPCANCEL) until stuck.
     2. Apply deterministic congruence normalization (LLL + lex tie-break on p).
     3. (Optional) Apply prime-local block normalization (Jordan decomposition + sorted blocks), including
        truncations Πℓr when desired.

9. Notes on conventions
     • The precise mapping from vertex parameters (av , bv ) to (ev , (p)v ) depends on orientation/
       normalization conventions for SO(4)-bundles over S 4 . The rewrite calculus itself is invariant under
       any consistent choice, as long as:


     • Q is integral symmetric and transforms by congruence under slides,


     • p transforms as p ↦ U −1 p, and

     • stabilizations append zeros to p.


     • If you adopt a different convention, record it once and keep it fixed; NF(Q, p) remains well-defined
       for that conventioer
