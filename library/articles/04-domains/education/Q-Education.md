Pillars (the “why + guardrails”)
   ●​ Do-first, name-later math: learners manipulate flows, braids, and boxes, then attach
      formal words later. They toggle between three frames—Geometry (G), Number (N), and
      Information/Quantum (Q). The curriculum centers on generators & moves (compose,
      parallel, swap, copy/merge when allowed) and what invariants emerge from those
      moves.​

   ●​ Ethics + emotional safety by design: a Conscious Sovereignty Layer (CSL) runs in
      the background to keep challenge inside safe bounds, with a Reciprocity Engine for
      peer energy/exchange, and a πL̄ıla growth tracker for visible milestones. This is
      trauma-aware, SEL-rooted, and neurodiversity-supportive.​

   ●​ Spiral by age band: formal tiering ensures concepts are age-appropriate and build
      recursively (Grades 3–5, 6–8, 9–12).​




Age-banded pathway (10+)
Band A — Ages 10–12: Quantum Sense-Making
Goal: intuitive quantum “habits of mind” via flows, fractions, symmetry, and randomness that
map cleanly into quantum/information frames.

   ●​ Labs & moves​

           ○​ Pipe & Marble Labs (conservation), Paper Braids (swaps), Area as
              multiplicity (parallel wires).​

           ○​ Introduce chance as processes and Markov walk mats as a bridge to
              amplitudes later.​

   ●​ Framing: keep toggling G↔N↔Q to normalize that “the same idea” looks different in
      each frame.​

   ●​ Reflection protocol: short, weekly G→E→C (Generator→Enhancer→Critic)
      loops—students try, improve with constraints, then critique—tracked with lightweight
      metrics.​
Band B — Ages 12–14: Quantum Literacy
Goal: move from intuition to structure—stochastic/linear boxes, symmetry, early information
ideas.

   ●​ Content​

           ○​ Chance boxes (rows sum to 1), linear boxes (slope as gain), orbit diagrams
              for invariants.​

           ○​ No-cloning as a rule of the region: in the “quantum region” the copy node is
              forbidden—great bridge into information-theoretic limits.​

   ●​ Practice & assessment​

           ○​ Run 9×50–60 min “Loops & Metrics”: MML loop, peer triads, evidence vs.
              counter-evidence, ethics studio, and a mini-capstone—track PER
              (Prompt-Efficiency), MLEM (Meta-Learning Efficiency), and trend TCI
              (convergence).​



Band C — Ages 14–18: Linear Worlds to Quantum
Computing
Goal: formalize the diagrammatic “linear world” and offer a clean on-ramp to QC/QIS topics.

   ●​ Units: matrices as boxes; parallel = tensor; probability boxes; Fourier as a
      change-of-basis; coding & noise; capstone simplifies a mixed classical/quantum circuit
      with ZX-lite rewrites.​

   ●​ Quantum electives: quantization, tech applications, intro to qubits/gates (Shor/Grover at
      concept level), and ethical implications of quantum tech.​




Classroom model (how it runs)
   ●​ Weekly cadence: 1 period/week integrated into science/math, or 2-week intensives;
      uses classroom-safe AI with logging dashboards and offline alternatives where needed.​

   ●​ Studios/stations:​
        1.​ Flow Lab (marbles/snap-circuits), 2) Braid Table (paper/string swaps), 3) Info
            Corner (copy/merge rules; “no-clone” puzzles), 4) Reflection Bar (G→E→C
            loop with journals).​

 ●​ Inclusion & neurodiversity: EchoBraid-style resonance protocols (match input rhythm
    to cognition); CSL limits intensity; culturally responsive examples.​




Assessment & evidence (beyond tests)
 ●​ Recursive Assessment Engine (Grades 3–5, 6–8): score integrity of feedback loops
    over time; pair with student reflection.​

 ●​ MML metrics: PER (utility vs. prompt/effort), MLEM (insight per iteration time), TCI
    (convergence). Students keep iteration logs.​

 ●​ Lab integrity: uncertainty estimation, residuals, confidence intervals—students compare
    predictions to measurement.​




A 6-week starter unit (Ages 10–11)
 1.​ Flows & Fair Shares (G↔N): build split/merge systems; predict bulb brightness from
     flow multiplicity. Exit: show a diagram → build it.​

 2.​ Random Walks (N↔Q-proto): Markov walk mats; compare empirical frequencies. Exit:
     explain “chance as a process.”​

 3.​ Braids & Swaps (toward interference thinking): paper braids, minimal crossings; reason
     with allowed moves.​

 4.​ Copy vs. No-Clone (Information lens): run copy/merge in classical region; contrast the
     forbidden copy in the quantum region with puzzles.​

 5.​ Mini-investigation: students design a flow device that achieves a target split; predict,
     measure, discuss error.​

 6.​ Showcase: 3-iteration G→E→C improvement cycle; students present PER/MLEM
     deltas + a short ethics note on fairness/cooperation in their team.​
Teacher PD & rollout
  ●​ PD modules: convert axioms→moves; design G↔N↔Q tasks; lab safety; equity by
     embodiment.​

  ●​ Timeline: Semester 0 PD + pilot two grades; Semesters 1–2 full K–8 rollout; create a
     local lab hub; public expo.​




What makes this “quantum pedagogy” (not just “cute
physics”)?
  ●​ It grounds later QC/QIS in linear-diagram thinking and information constraints kids
     can do with their hands first.​

  ●​ It bakes in ethics & emotional regulation through CSL/Reciprocity/πL̄ıla so challenge
     scales safely—crucial at 10–14.​

  ●​ It measures learning as a recursive process (PER/MLEM/TCI + lab uncertainty), not
     just right/wrong.
