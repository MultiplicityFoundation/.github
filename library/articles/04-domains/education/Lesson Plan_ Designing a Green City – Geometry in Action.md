**Lesson Plan: Designing a Green City -- Geometry in Action**
=============================================================

### **Introduction: Setting the Stage**

**Framing Philosophy:** As educators, we are guided by the understanding
that education is not a rigid staircase but a vibrant garden, growing in
all directions and at different rhythms. This lesson plan is rooted in
that philosophy, connecting the abstract principles of geometry with
tangible, real-world applications. By weaving together creativity,
collaboration, and ethical design, this project aligns with the Citizen
Gardens mission to bridge academic knowledge with social responsibility
and empower students to become thoughtful creators of their future
world.

**Lesson Overview:** In the \"Designing a Green City\" project,
8th-grade students will work in collaborative teams to design a
sustainable and inclusive city block. This hands-on project challenges
them to apply core geometric principles---such as area, perimeter,
volume, and transformations---to solve practical urban planning
problems. The lesson emphasizes not only mathematical accuracy but also
effective teamwork, creative design, and ethical reasoning about how
physical spaces can serve diverse communities and promote environmental
well-being.

### **1. Lesson Details**

  Core Information    Details
  ------------------- ---------------------------------------------
  **Grade Level**     8th Grade
  **Theme**           Preparing for the Future
  **Subject Focus**   Geometry, Urban Design, Sustainability
  **Duration**        2--3 class periods (modular and adjustable)

**Core Learning Objectives:**

-   Apply geometric concepts including area, perimeter, volume, and
    > transformations.

-   Collaborate effectively in groups, developing communication and
    > planning skills.

-   Use critical and ethical reasoning to design inclusive, sustainable
    > urban spaces.

### **2. ADHD-Inclusive Supports and Differentiated Strategies**

#### **🧠 Supports for ADHD Learners**

  Strategy                             Implementation in this Lesson
  ------------------------------------ ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  **Movement & Hands-On Learning**     Students build physical models using craft materials or LEGOs. Frequent movement breaks are structured into work periods.
  **Executive Function Scaffolding**   The project is broken into clear, manageable steps using visual agendas, timers, and specific task cards for each role.
  **Choice & Autonomy**                Students choose which part of the city block to design, such as parks, housing, or transportation systems.
  **Interest-Based Hooks**             The lesson begins with an engaging challenge: \"The city needs your help! We're redesigning it to be fair, sustainable, and beautiful---how can geometry help?\"
  **Collaboration**                    Teams use rotating roles (designer, measurer, planner, presenter) to provide structure and ensure shared responsibility.
  **Flexible Output**                  Students can demonstrate their learning through various formats, including physical models, digital designs, posters, or video walk-throughs.

#### **Differentiated Strategies for Diverse Learners**

-   **Visual & Kinesthetic Learners:**

    -   Use tangible models like building blocks or recycled materials
        > to construct geometric forms.

    -   Design garden plots using shapes like hexagons to explore area
        > and perimeter in a hands-on way.

-   **Auditory Learners:**

    -   Participate in \"Geometry Story Circles\" to narrate the design
        > process using geometric vocabulary.

    -   Engage in small-group math talks to discuss different approaches
        > to solving design and calculation problems.

-   **Logical-Mathematical Learners:**

    -   Connect the project to larger systems by redesigning a real
        > space, like the schoolyard, using geometric optimization.

    -   Solve integrated logic puzzles or design challenges that require
        > precise calculations of volume, area, and angles.

-   **Artistic & Creative Learners:**

    -   Explore geometric art forms like mandalas or fractals and apply
        > principles of symmetry and transformation to their city
        > designs.

    -   Use storyboarding or animation tools to visualize and narrate
        > the geometric transformations used in their park or building
        > layouts.

These varied approaches honor the principle of Multiplicity, recognizing
that students express geometric understanding through diverse cognitive
and creative pathways.

### **4. Lesson Outline: A 3-Day Plan**

#### **Day 1: Introduction & Planning (Total Time: 45 min)**

1.  **Hook Activity (10 min):** Present the core challenge to students:
    > *\"Your city needs redesigning to be more sustainable, inclusive,
    > and green. How can geometry help?\"* Show compelling visual
    > examples of green city features like rooftop gardens, innovative
    > bike paths, and buildings with solar panels to inspire them.

2.  **Form Teams (5 min):** Organize students into small groups.
    > Distribute the four specific role cards to each team to provide
    > structure and clarify responsibilities:

    -   **Designer:** Leads the visual and creative aspects of the
        > layout.

    -   **Measurer/Calculator:** Responsible for all geometric
        > calculations.

    -   **Planner/Scheduler:** Manages time, tasks, and keeps the team
        > on track.

    -   **Presenter:** Prepares to share the team\'s final design and
        > reasoning.

3.  **Plan City Block (30 min):** Teams begin the design process by
    > sketching a layout of their city block. They should label
    > different zones (e.g., residential, green space, water feature)
    > and begin calculating rough dimensions using area and perimeter
    > formulas. All sketches, notes, and calculations must be recorded
    > in a group **\"City Planning Log.\"**

#### **Day 2: Build & Calculate (Total Time: 60 min)**

1.  **Work Time (45 min):** This is the primary hands-on construction
    > phase. Students use the provided materials to build their physical
    > or digital models based on their Day 1 plans. Encourage students
    > to see their city block as an interconnected system, where each
    > geometric choice affects the whole. As they build, they must apply
    > and document the following geometric principles:

    -   **Area and Perimeter:** Calculate the total green space, road
        > surfaces, and building footprints.

    -   **Volume:** Determine the capacity of buildings, water tanks, or
        > other 3D features.

    -   **Transformations:** Use rotation, reflection, or translation to
        > create symmetry or repeating patterns in park layouts or
        > housing arrangements.

    -   **Pythagorean Theorem:** Calculate the length of diagonal paths
        > or sightlines across open spaces.

2.  **Check-in & Reflect (15 min):** Use timers and checklists to help
    > groups monitor their progress. This is an ideal time for a teacher
    > check-in with each group to offer support. An *optional
    > movement/stretch break every 20 minutes* is recommended to help
    > students stay focused and engaged.

#### **Day 3: Present & Reflect (Total Time: 60 min)**

1.  **Final Touches (15 min):** Teams use this time to finalize their
    > models, double-check their calculations in the City Planning Log,
    > and prepare for their 2-minute group presentation.

2.  **Group Presentations (30 min):** Each team presents their city
    > block design to the class. The Presenter leads, but all members
    > should contribute to explaining their geometric choices and the
    > ethical reasoning behind their design (e.g., how it promotes
    > sustainability or inclusivity). An optional **\"gallery walk\"**
    > format can be used, where teams rotate to view and discuss each
    > other\'s models.

3.  **Empathy Circles (15 min):** Conclude the project with a reflective
    > discussion in small groups or as a whole class. Guide the
    > reflection with the following key questions:

    -   How did our design help different types of people?

    -   What was hardest about working as a team?

    -   What would you improve in your next design?

### **5. Assessment and Evaluation**

A multi-faceted assessment approach, aligned with Multiplicity Theory,
provides a holistic view of student learning, valuing the interconnected
process as much as the final product.

#### **Assessment Rubric**

  Criteria                  4 - Exceeds                                                               3 - Meets                                           2 - Approaching                                 1 - Beginning
  ------------------------- ------------------------------------------------------------------------- --------------------------------------------------- ----------------------------------------------- ---------------------------------------
  **Geometry Accuracy**     All required calculations are accurate and insightful.                    Most calculations are accurate with minor errors.   Some major calculation errors are present.      Limited or incorrect use of geometry.
  **Collaboration**         Excellent teamwork, with all roles actively and effectively fulfilled.    Good teamwork, with most roles fulfilled.           Participation is uneven within the group.       Poor collaboration and communication.
  **Creativity & Design**   The design is highly creative, thoughtful, and well-organized.            The design is practical and clearly laid out.       Some design elements are present but unclear.   The design is incomplete or unclear.
  **Ethical Thinking**      The design shows a strong connection to both sustainability and equity.   Clear ethical considerations are evident.           Some ethical reasoning is present.              Limited or no connection to ethics.

#### **Additional Assessment Methods**

-   **Project Portfolios:** Collect each team\'s \"City Planning Log,\"
    > which should include sketches, calculations, photos of the model,
    > and journal entries. This provides evidence of the team\'s process
    > and growth in understanding.

-   **Self & Peer Assessment:** Have students reflect on their own
    > contributions and the effectiveness of their teamwork using
    > guiding questions like, *\"How did I contribute?\"* and *\"What
    > did I learn about working with others?\"*

### **6. Optional Extensions**

-   **Professional Connections:** Compare the student-designed city
    > blocks to real-world city blueprints to learn about professional
    > urban design standards and practices.

-   **Guest Speaker:** Invite a local urban planner, architect, or
    > environmental designer to speak to the class about their work and
    > provide feedback on the student projects.

-   **Augmented Reality:** Use AR apps to let students overlay their
    > digital designs onto the classroom floor, creating an immersive
    > experience of their green city.
