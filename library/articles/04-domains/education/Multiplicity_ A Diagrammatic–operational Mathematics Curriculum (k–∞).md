Multiplicity: A Diagrammatic--Operational Mathematics Curriculum (K--∞)
=======================================================================

*A do-first, name-later foundation for mathematics grounded in diagrams,
moves, and multiplicity. Designed for ages 4 through research level.*

0) Philosophy & Design Constraints
----------------------------------

**Mathematical Onticity.** Mathematics is not a description but the
lawful dynamics of reality. We therefore start with **operations**
(moves) rather than axioms or sentences about them.

**Frame Relativity (MRP).** Every topic is presented in three isomorphic
frames: (G) geometry of wires and surfaces, (N) number/combinatorics of
counts and multiplicities, (Q) information/energy flow (quantum &
probabilistic circuits). Learners toggle frames by diagrammatic
translation.

**Experimental Falsifiability.** Each unit culminates in a measurable
prediction (e.g., Q-factors, frequency shifts, combinatorial counts),
connecting formal structure to laboratory artifacts (acoustics, optics,
electronics, fluid/sand tables, RF cavities, and simulation).

**No-axiom Syntax.** Foundations are specified by **generators & moves**
(rewrite rules) rather than verbal axioms. Correctness = being reachable
by allowed rewrites. Proofs = **normal forms** and **invariants** of the
rewrite system.

1) Core Grammar of Mathematics (for all levels)
-----------------------------------------------

We fix a minimal **diagrammatic grammar** used across the curriculum.

### Generators (things you can draw)

-   **Wires**: oriented lines carrying "stuff."
-   **Dots (•)** on wires: events (fork/join/copy/delete).
-   **Boxes**: processes with input wires (bottom) and output wires
    (top).
-   **Regions**: areas labeling contexts (e.g., probability, vector
    space, mod p).

### Primitive Moves (legal rewrites)

1.  **Serial compose**: stack boxes (do-then-do).
2.  **Parallel compose**: place side-by-side (do-in-parallel).
3.  **Bend/slide**: planar isotopy without cutting.
4.  **Copy/merge**: permitted at labeled dots when the region allows it.
5.  **Discard/unit**: erase a wire into a ground symbol (allowed only in
    classical/probabilistic regions).
6.  **Swap**: cross wires (braid) when allowed.

### What *emerges* without stating axioms

-   Identity (straight wire), associativity (re-bracketing by sliding),
    monoidal product (side-by-side), symmetry/braiding (crossings),
    duals (bends/cups/caps), probability normalization (total flow = 1
    in stochastic region), linearity (boxes represent linear maps when
    copying is restricted).

### Multiplicity & Primes (recurring strand)

-   **Indecomposables (primes)** = boxes/wires that cannot be expressed
    as a nontrivial serial ∘ or parallel ⊗ decomposition by allowed
    moves.
-   **Multiplicity** of a component = the number of parallel copies in
    its normal form.
-   **Spectral view**: each diagram has a multiset of eigenmodes;
    *multiplicity* counts degeneracies of modes under allowed
    symmetries.

Learners never read axioms; they **practice moves** and **observe
invariants**.

2) Spiral Scope & Sequence (K--∞)
---------------------------------

Each band lists **Core Competencies**, **Key Activities**,
**Assessment**, and **Artifacts**.

### A. Early Childhood (Ages 4--6): "Count, Connect, Compose"

**Competencies** - Recognize and build numbers by **concatenation of
ticks** and by **bundling** (5-as-■■■■■, 10-as-two-bundles-of-5). -
Understand addition as "putting strings together," multiplication as
"repeat a block." - Identify **same vs different** by allowed moves
(slide/rotate without tearing).

**Activities** - **String Town**: yarn wires, wooden boxes; children
compose processes physically. - **Move Bingo**: draw two pictures; if
one can be slid into the other, shout "same!" - **Prime Blocks**: blocks
that only split along certain seams; children discover indecomposable
shapes.

**Assessment** - Performance tasks: can the child transform diagram A
into B using legal moves? - Journals: picture stories showing
"do-then-do."

**Artifacts** - Photo portfolios of rewrites, number walls of
concatenations, "prime zoo" of indecomposable blocks.

### B. Upper Primary (Ages 7--10): "Flows & Fractions"

**Competencies** - Translate between (G,N,Q) frames: area of rectangles
↔ repeated concatenation ↔ parallel/serial processes. - Fractions as
**sharing flows**: split a wire into equal parts; equalize by merge. -
Symmetry by **move sets**: flip/rotate puzzles define a group of moves
without naming it.

**Activities** - **Pipe & Marble Labs**: split/merge flows, measure
conservation. - **Paper Braids**: crossings represent swaps; count
minimal crossings. - **Area as Multiplicity**: tiles-as-parallel wires;
perimeter as boundary length (invariant under certain moves).

**Assessment** - Practical quizzes with snap-circuit boards: build a
given flow; predict brightness (proportional to flow multiplicity).

**Artifacts** - "Flow passports" documenting conserved quantities; braid
posters.

### C. Middle School (Ages 11--13): "Symmetry, Randomness, & Ratios"

**Competencies** - Model chance as **stochastic boxes**: rows sum to 1;
serial/parallel composition of chance. - Identify invariants under
allowed symmetries; classify with **orbit diagrams**. - Linear functions
as boxes; slope = gain; intercept = background flow.

**Activities** - **Markov Walk Mats**: floor tiles are states; dice
choose transitions; estimate stationary distributions by time-on-tiles.
- **Polygon Moves**: allowed flips/rotations generate congruence;
discover minimal presentations of move-sets. - **Harmonic Strings**:
measure frequencies; note integer ratios and **prime factors**; record
multiplicities of modes.

**Assessment** - Lab reports with predictions vs measurements; error
bars introduced.

**Artifacts** - Class Markov chain murals with measured stationary
frequencies; frequency vs length graphs (f ∝ 1/L).

### D. Secondary (Ages 14--18): "Linear Worlds, Groups, and Information"

**Competencies** - Work fluently with **string diagrams** for linear
algebra: matrices as boxes; vector spaces as labeled regions; tensor
product as parallel. - Solve systems with **diagrammatic Gaussian
elimination** (spider fusion / copy-merge laws). - Understand
**groups-as-moves** and **group actions** on sets and diagrams; compute
orbits/stabilizers via pictures. - Reason about **information**:
classical copying vs no-cloning (copy node forbidden in quantum region).
- Introduce **complex numbers** and **Fourier transform** as a
change-of-basis box; visualize convolution as wire-merge.

**Activities** - **ZX/ZW-lite Calculus**: color-coded copy/merge nodes;
simplify circuits to normal forms. - **Error-Correcting Codes as
Tilings**: parity checks are boxes; syndrome wires indicate invariants.
- **Optics Lab**: slits and lenses as boxes; convolution vs
multiplication duality. - **Electronic RLC Labs**: measure transfer
function \|H(ω)\|; identify resonant peaks and multiplicities.

**Assessment** - Mixed-mode exams: (a) diagram rewrite sequences, (b)
lab predictions with uncertainties, (c) short formal proofs by
invariants.

**Artifacts** - Simplified quantum/classical circuits; code murals; lab
notebooks with Bode plots and fitted Q-factors.

### E. Undergraduate Core (Tier I): "Categories, Probability, and Spectra"

**Competencies** - Formalize the grammar as a **strict symmetric
monoidal category** via generators/relations (still presented as moves).
- Map between diagrams and algebra: functors to **(Vect, ⊗)**, **(Stoch,
⊗)**, **(Rel, ×)**. - Compute spectra: eigenvalues/eigenmodes of boxes;
interpret **multiplicity** as degeneracy. - Probability in diagrams:
**Markov categories**; Bayesian inversion as a diagrammatic adjoint.

**Activities** - **Functorial Semantics Studio**: same diagram evaluated
in different target categories; observe what changes and what doesn't. -
**Spectral Labs**: discrete Laplacians on graphs; measure eigenvalue
multiplicities vs symmetries. - **Information Flow Practicum**: build
probabilistic models; perform updates as string rewrites.

**Assessments & Artifacts** - Problem sets translating between pictures
and matrices; lab reports comparing predicted symmetries to
multiplicities in spectra.

### F. Advanced Undergraduate / Graduate (Tier II): "Primes, Zeta, and Physical Cavities"

**Competencies** - Indecomposables via **Krull--Schmidt** in
diagrammatic categories (when applicable). Prime factorization of
processes under ∘ and ⊗. - **Generating functions** of diagrams: ζ\_D(s)
:= ∑\_{λ∈Spec(D)} λ\^{-s}; connect poles/zeros to growth/decay of
counts. - **Operator calculus**: adjoints, norms, and bounds on
composition; stability under perturbation. - **Quantum circuits** in
diagrammatic form; no-cloning as structural prohibition.

**Activities** - **Fractal Cavities**: 3D-print resonators with
prime-controlled geometries; measure mode multiplicities and Q. Compare
with scaling law (see §4.3). - **Casimir Toy Models**: parallel plate
and corrugated cavity simulations; predict shifts based on spectral
density changes. - **ZX-calculus Projects**: simplify quantum algorithms
diagrammatically; verify equivalence by rewrite reachability.

**Assessments & Artifacts** - Research-style reports; conference-style
posters; repositories with simulations and measured datasets.

### G. Research Studio / Doctoral: "Multiplicity Operators & Testable Predictions"

**Competencies** - Define and analyze **multiplicity operators** on
process algebras; prove norm and convergence bounds. - Relate spectral
multiplicities to **symmetry groups** via character theory and to
**prime factorizations** via length spectra. - Design falsifiable
experiments linking diagrammatic primitives to physically measurable
invariants (Q, frequency shifts, noise floors).

**Activities** - **Operator Inequalities**: derive submultiplicativity
and stability bounds. - **Zeta of a Diagram**: study analytic
continuation heuristics and numerical evidence; connect to gaps and
oscillations in counts. - **BEC / RF Labs (with partners)**: investigate
mode crowding and multiplicity control via geometry.

**Assessments & Artifacts** - Manuscript-quality papers with error
analyses and preregistered protocols.

3) Cross-Cutting Strands
------------------------

-   **Computation**: every level uses block-based or textual programming
    to implement rewrites; higher levels implement automated theorem

-   **Language**: storytelling from diagrams to sentences; later, LaTeX

-   **Art & Music**: braids as dance notation; harmonics and prime
    rhythms; tiling patterns.
-   **Civic Science**: open-data labs; publish class datasets and
    replicate across schools.

4) Formal Cores (rigor without axioms-in-words)
-----------------------------------------------

### 4.1 Rewriting System (Foundation)

Let 𝔻 be the free string-diagram category generated by boxes {B\_i} and
structural nodes {copy, merge, swap, unit, counit} subject to planar
isotopy and local relations (spider fusion, yanking). **Proofs** are
sequences of local rewrites terminating in a **normal form** NF(·)
unique up to planar isotopy.

**Soundness.** If D →\* E by rewrites, then F(D) = F(E) for any monoidal
functor F into a model category (Vect, Stoch, Rel,...).

**Completeness (contextual).** For the ZX-lite fragment on qubits, the
rewrite system is complete for circuit equality within the fragment used
in coursework (bounded angle set). Students verify by normalization to a
canonical form.

### 4.2 Multiplicity Operator

For a diagram D with spectral multiset Spec(D) = {λ\_k} (eigenvalues of
its linear semantics), define the **multiplicity profile**

\[ \_p(D) := \#{}, p . \]

Define the **total multiplicity** M(D) = ∑\_k m\_k where m\_k is
algebraic multiplicity of λ\_k. In symmetry-rich families, M(D)
organizes by prime-power orbits.

**Submultiplicativity under composition.** For bounded operators A,B, \[
M(AB) M(A),M(B). \] Sketch: algebraic multiplicities add within
invariant subspaces; composition cannot increase the product of
independent orbit counts beyond Cartesian product size. (Formal proof
assigned in Tier II.)

### 4.3 Spectral--Geometric Prediction (for labs)

Consider a family of resonators {C\_α} with boundary conditions
admitting a discrete spectrum {ω\_{α,i}} with quality factors Q\_{α,i}.
Define prime-weighted complexity of the cavity shape by \[ (α) := \_{p }
p\^{*p(α)}, \] where ν\_p(α) counts p-gonal or p-fold symmetric motifs
in the cavity mesh. **Prediction:** \[ i, Q{α,i} K, (α)\^{} (\>0 ), \]
with deviations controlled by roughness ε and loss tangent δ: \[ Q*{α,i}
- c\_3. \] Students fit (K,β,c\_1,c\_2,c\_3) to data from 3D-printed
cavities and test generalization on held-out shapes.

### 4.4 Error & Uncertainty

Given repeated measurements Q\_i, estimate (Q), s, and the standard
error (=s/). For frequency shifts Δf under a perturbation with predicted
effect Δf\_pred, report residuals r\_i=Δf\_i-Δf\_pred, check normality
(QQ-plot), and compute 95% CI via t\_{n-1}.

5) Representative Syllabi
-------------------------

### 5.1 Grade 3: "Flows, Fractions, and Symmetry" (10 weeks)

-   **Week 1--2:** Tick strings → addition/multiplication; area as
    multiplicity (tilings).
-   **Week 3:** Flow splitting; equal shares; conservation tasks.
-   **Week 4--5:** Symmetry moves; wallpaper patterns; classify by
    allowed moves.
-   **Week 6:** Random walk games; empirical frequencies.
-   **Week 7--8:** Braids and swaps; minimal crossing puzzles.
-   **Week 9:** Project: Design a fountain manifold of pipes achieving a
    target split.
-   **Week 10:** Exhibition & oral demonstration (move-justified
    equivalences).

**Assessment rubric (excerpt)** - Correctness of moves (0--3),
identification of invariants (0--3), clarity of explanation by showing
**how** not **why** (0--3), reflection on errors (0--1).

### 5.2 Secondary: "Linear Diagrams & Information" (14 weeks)

-   **Weeks 1--2:** Boxes-as-matrices; parallel = tensor; serial =
    product.
-   **Weeks 3--4:** Diagrammatic elimination; solve Ax=b; interpret rank
    as number of independent wires.
-   **Weeks 5--6:** Probability boxes; Bayes as wire-bending (cup/cap
    adjunction).
-   **Weeks 7--8:** Fourier box; convolution = merge; uncertainty
    trade-offs.
-   **Weeks 9--10:** Groups-as-moves; orbits & stabilizers; counting via
    Burnside (picture method).
-   **Weeks 11--12:** Coding diagrams; parity checks; noise channels.
-   **Weeks 13--14:** Capstone: simplify a mixed classical/quantum
    circuit with ZX-lite rewrites.

**Deliverables:** problem sets, two labs (optics, electronics), final
poster.

### 5.3 Undergraduate Core: "Categories & Spectra" (12 weeks)

-   Generators/relations; free monoidal categories.
-   Functorial semantics into Vect/Stoch/Rel.
-   Graph Laplacians; eigenvalue multiplicity vs symmetry.
-   Zeta-of-a-diagram; growth exponents.
-   Final: empirical test of multiplicity--Q relation on cavity or graph
    families.

6) Laboratory Tracks
--------------------

### 6.1 Low-Cost K--12 Labs

-   **String & Pipe Kits** (yarn, PVC): flows, conservation, splitting.
-   **Paper Optics**: slits from foil; lens kits; observe convolution vs
    multiplication.
-   **Snap Circuits / Arduino**: implement boxes; measure gain;
    introduce noise.

### 6.2 Advanced Labs (Tier II and above)

-   **3D-Printed Fractal Cavities**: variable symmetry counts; measure
    resonances with microphones/RF VNAs.
-   **Casimir Analogues**: vibrating membranes with patterned
    boundaries; track spectral shifts.
-   **BEC/Cold-Atom Simulations**: numerical GP equation; visualize mode
    multiplicities.

**Data Standards:** all labs publish CSV with schema: geometry
descriptors (including prime motif counts), material params, measured
spectrum, estimated Q, uncertainty.

7) Assessment Architecture
--------------------------

-   **Formative**: rewrite journaling, gallery walks of transformations,
    whiteboard interviews ("show me the moves").
-   **Summative**: practical builds, diagram-to-algebra translations,
    lab fits with uncertainty.
-   **Competency Badges**
    -   *Diagrammatic Composer* (can normalize processes),
    -   *Symmetry Cartographer* (classifies by moves),
    -   *Spectral Analyst* (extracts modes & multiplicities),
    -   *Information Alchemist* (translates between classical/quantum
        regions with correct structural constraints).

Rubrics emphasize **reachability** (can you legally rewrite A→B?),
**invariance recognition**, and **measurement literacy**.

8) Teacher Preparation & Materials
----------------------------------

**Essentials**: rope sets, magnetic whiteboards with snap-on nodes, card
decks for boxes, braid cords, simple optics kit, snap circuits, laptops
with diagram editor & Python/Julia notebooks.

**PD Modules** 1. *From Axioms to Moves*: converting standard lessons
into rewrite challenges. 2. *Multiple Frames*: designing tasks that
demand G↔N↔Q translations. 3. *Lab Integrity*: uncertainty estimation
and pre-registration. 4. *Equity by Embodiment*: kinesthetic entry
points and multilingual diagrams.

**Safety**: standard maker-lab protocols; PPE for soldering/printing; RF
exposure limits for advanced labs.

9) Sample Tasks & Solutions (excerpts)
--------------------------------------

**Task (Middle School).** Two splitters in series split 1 unit flow into
thirds. Show by diagrams that order doesn't matter for total shares.
*Solution sketch:* show commutation by sliding merge/split nodes
(isotopy), compute 1→1/3 on each branch in either order.

**Task (Secondary).** Simplify a diagram corresponding to a 3×3 linear
system by spider fusion to find rank. *Solution sketch:* fuse green copy
nodes; count remaining independent wires = rank.

**Task (Undergrad).** A graph with D₄ symmetry exhibits an eigenvalue of
multiplicity 2. Predict this from orbits. *Solution sketch:* irreps of
D₄ include two 1D and one 2D; the 2D irrep explains multiplicity-2 mode.

**Task (Tier II).** For cavity family with ν₃=2, ν₅=1, other ν\_p=0,
predict median Q ∝ 3^{2β}·5^{β}. Fit β from pilot data.

**Task (Doctoral).** Prove (M(AB) M(A)M(B)) for algebraic multiplicity
under stated hypotheses; discuss equality conditions when eigenbases
align.

11) Outcomes & Research Questions
---------------------------------

-   Do students transfer between G/N/Q frames more fluidly than control
    curricula?
-   Does diagrammatic normalization improve proof-writing later?
-   Is the predicted link between prime motif counts and Q robust across
    materials and scales?
-   What minimal move-sets achieve completeness for targeted fragments
    (ZX-lite, Markov categories)?

**Success Metrics:** normalized gains on conceptual inventories adapted
to diagrammatic reasoning; replication of lab scaling laws with
pre-registered error models; publication output of research studios.

12) Appendices
--------------

### A. Minimal Symbol Legend

-   straight wire = identity;
-   box = linear/prob/stochastic map;
-   green dot = copy/merge;
-   red dot = plus/minus fuse;
-   cup/cap = state/effect;
-   cross = swap;
-   ⊗ = parallel; ∘ = serial.

### B. Diagram-to-Algebra Dictionary

-   wire labeled X ↔ vector space or set X
-   box f:X→Y ↔ matrix/probability kernel f
-   parallel ↔ tensor product (or Cartesian product in Rel)
-   bend ↔ evaluation/coevaluation
-   copy ↔ comonoid; merge ↔ monoid

### C. Software & Templates

-   Diagram editor, normalization checker, lab notebooks with
    uncertainty calculators, CSV schema.

**This curriculum is designed to be lived in classrooms, labs, and
studios. The "axioms" are the moves learners practice. Theorems are the
invariants they discover.**
