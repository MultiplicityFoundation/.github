\section*{Executive Summary}

% ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Phase Mirror is a multi-layered governance oracle for software development
organizations. Built on \textbf{Multiplicity Theory}---a prime-indexed,
recursively stable mathematical framework---it models governance not as a static
policy tree but as a recursively generated pattern of prime-labeled interactions
across architectural layers. The system's core primitive is the \emph{Mirror
Dissonance Protocol (PMD)}: rather than resolving contradiction, Phase Mirror
\textbf{names} it, surfacing productive tensions between autonomy and governance,
accuracy and compliance, velocity and safety, as enumerable, owner-bound levers.

Microsoft's \textbf{Agent Governance Toolkit (AGT)} (v3.2.1, MIT License) provides
deterministic runtime policy enforcement for AI agents: sub-millisecond
policy evaluation, zero-trust cryptographic identity (Ed25519 + ML-DSA-65),
execution sandboxing via four-tier privilege rings, and full coverage of all
ten OWASP Agentic risks. AGT's stated violation rate under deterministic
enforcement is $0.00\%$ compared to a $26.67\%$ violation rate under prompt-based
safety.

This report documents:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item \textbf{The resolved integration architecture}: AGT halts at the
    \emph{tool-call boundary} (pre-execution); Phase Mirror surfaces at the
    \emph{architectural boundary} (pre-ratification). These are
    non-overlapping checkpoints on the same governance trajectory.

  \item \textbf{A phased ADR roadmap} (Phase 0--3) with full file scaffold, CI
    integration sequences, and artifact specifications.
  \item \textbf{The mathematical foundations} of Phase Mirror under Multiplicity
    Theory, including the L0 invariant formal system, the PMD loop, and the
    trust-scoring algebra extended by AGT's identity model.
  \item \textbf{The Shadow AI registry protocol} resolving the divergent-inventory
    risk introduced by operating two agent discovery subsystems simultaneously.

The smallest viable next step is committing \texttt{governance/adrs/} infrastructure
(Phase 0, Days 1--7) and the dual MCP surface scan gate (Phase 1, Days 7--30).
All subsequent phases depend on these two artifacts as binding constraints.

\subsection{Multiplicity Theory}

Multiplicity Theory constitutes a prime-indexed, recursively stable mathematical
framework wherein \emph{multiplicity}---the recurrence of elements understood
through prime number decomposition---forms the basis for modeling nonlinear,
emergent structures across mathematics, physics, computation, and cognition.

\begin{definition}[Multiplicity Space]
A \emph{multiplicity space} $\mathcal{M} = (S, \Pi, \phi, \rho)$ consists of:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $S$: a base set of elements (states, rules, agents, invariants)
  \item $\Pi = \{p_1, p_2, p_3, \ldots\}$: the ordered sequence of prime numbers
    serving as structural indices
  \item $\phi: S \times \Pi \rightarrow S$: a prime-indexed interaction map
  \item $\rho: S \rightarrow S$: a recursive feedback operator satisfying
    $\rho^n(s) = \rho(\rho^{n-1}(s))$ for all $s \in S$, $n \geq 1$
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Recursive Stability]
A multiplicity space is \emph{recursively stable} if for all $s \in S$ and
all $p_i \in \Pi$:
\[
  \lim_{n \to \infty} \rho^n(\phi(s, p_i)) \in S
\]
That is, iterated recursive feedback preserves membership in the base set.
\end{definition}

Phase Mirror instantiates Multiplicity Theory by treating governance rules
(MD-001 through MD-005 and L0 invariants L0-001 through L0-005) as
prime-indexed interaction patterns whose recursive application across
organizational repositories produces emergent governance signals.

\subsection{The Mirror Dissonance Protocol (PMD)}

The PMD loop is the operational core of Phase Mirror. It is a five-step
recursive cycle:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{PMD-\arabic*}]
  \item \textbf{Ingest}: receive artifacts (files, commits, PRs, ADRs)
  \item \textbf{L0 Gate}: validate all five L0 invariants; fail-closed on any violation
  \item \textbf{Rule Evaluation}: apply MD-001 through MD-005 (and extended rule tiers)
    against the artifact set
  \item \textbf{FP Calibration}: consult the False Positive store; apply
    contraction witness validation
  \item \textbf{Surface}: emit a \texttt{decision} $\in \{\texttt{pass},
    \texttt{warn}, \texttt{block}\}$ with full evidence chain and lever
    recommendations
\end{enumerate}

The PMD loop does \emph{not} resolve dissonance. It names it. The resolution
authority belongs to human architects operating through ADRs and the
$\Xi$-Constitutional documents.

\section{The AGT Integration: Resolving the Central Tension}
% ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

\begin{tensionbox}
\textbf{Phase Mirror is a surfacing oracle} (names dissonance, refuses to paper
over it). \textbf{AGT is a runtime enforcer} (deterministic policy evaluation
at action-execution time, $< 0.1\,\text{ms}$, between agent and action).

The risk: pulling AGT in could conflate ``surfacing productive contradictions''
with ``silently blocking actions''---exactly the fake consensus Phase Mirror
exists to resist.
\end{tensionbox}

\subsection{Resolution: Layered, Non-Overlapping Checkpoints}

The answer to the precision question \emph{``inform or halt?''} is
\textbf{both, but layered differently by scope}:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{AGT halts} at the \emph{tool-call boundary}: before the action
    executes, before a Copilot agent receives a Phase Mirror tool definition.
  \item \textbf{Phase Mirror surfaces} at the \emph{architectural boundary}:
    before intent is ratified, before an ADR is merged, before a deploy proceeds.
\end{itemize}

These are non-overlapping checkpoints on the same governance trajectory.
The conflation risk materializes only if AGT's policy engine is allowed to
silently pass ambiguous cases---which is exactly why
\texttt{agt verify --evidence --strict} must be the CI gate, not a soft advisory.
The \texttt{--strict} flag is load-bearing: without it, AGT can produce passing
results with insufficient evidence, reproducing the fake-consensus pattern
Phase Mirror exists to prevent.

\subsection{L0 Invariant Formal System}

The L0 foundation layer is the non-negotiable substrate of Phase Mirror.
All five invariants must pass before any higher-layer analysis proceeds.
Failure is fail-closed: immediate \texttt{block}, no further processing.

Let $\mathcal{A}$ denote the set of artifacts under analysis at time $t$.

\begin{invariant}[Schema Hash Integrity, L0-001]
\label{inv:schema-hash}
Let $H: \{0,1\}^* \rightarrow \{0,1\}^{256}$ be SHA-256. For schema content
$c \in \mathcal{A}$ and expected hash $h^* \in \{0,1\}^{256}$:
\[
  \text{L0-001}(c, h^*) = \begin{cases}
    \top & \text{if } H(c) = h^* \\
    \bot & \text{otherwise}
  \end{cases}
\]
Timing constraint: $\mathbb{E}[\tau_{\text{L0-001}}] \leq 12\,\text{ns}$,
$p_{99}(\tau_{\text{L0-001}}) \leq 50\,\text{ns}$.
\end{invariant}

\begin{invariant}[Permission Bits, L0-002]
\label{inv:permission-bits}
Let $\mathcal{W}$ be the set of GitHub Actions workflow files.
Define $\text{excessive}(w) = 1$ iff workflow $w$ contains
\texttt{permissions: write-all} or equivalent over-broad scope.
\[
  \text{L0-002}(\mathcal{W}) = \begin{cases}
    \top & \text{if } \forall w \in \mathcal{W}:\; \text{excessive}(w) = 0 \\
    \bot & \text{otherwise}
  \end{cases}
\]
\end{invariant}

\begin{invariant}[Drift Magnitude, L0-003]
\label{inv:drift}
Let $\mu_{\text{curr}}$ and $\mu_{\text{base}}$ be current and baseline
metric values with $\mu_{\text{base}} \neq 0$. For threshold $\delta \in (0,1)$
(default $\delta = 0.5$):
\[
  \text{L0-003}(\mu_{\text{curr}}, \mu_{\text{base}}, \delta) = \begin{cases}
    \top & \text{if } \dfrac{|\mu_{\text{curr}} - \mu_{\text{base}}|}{\mu_{\text{base}}} \leq \delta \\[6pt]
    \bot & \text{otherwise}
  \end{cases}
\]
\end{invariant}

\begin{invariant}[Nonce Freshness, L0-004]
\label{inv:nonce}
Let $n$ be a cryptographic nonce with generation timestamp $t_n$,
current time $t_{\text{now}}$, and maximum age $\tau_{\max} = 3600\,\text{s}$
(one hour):
\[
  \text{L0-004}(n, t_n, t_{\text{now}}) = \begin{cases}
    \top & \text{if } t_{\text{now}} - t_n \leq \tau_{\max} \\
    \bot & \text{otherwise (replay attack vector)}
  \end{cases}
\]
\end{invariant}

\begin{invariant}[Contraction Witness, L0-005]
\label{inv:contraction}
Let $r_{\text{prev}}, r_{\text{curr}} \in [0,1]$ be previous and current
false-positive rates (FPR). Let $E = \{e_1, \ldots, e_k\}$ be the set of
witnessed FP events, with $k_{\min} = 10$ the minimum evidence count.
Let $\text{reviewed}(E) = |\{e \in E : e.\text{reviewedBy} \neq \emptyset\}|$.
\[
  \text{L0-005}(r_{\text{prev}}, r_{\text{curr}}, E) = \begin{cases}
    \top & \text{if } r_{\text{curr}} \geq r_{\text{prev}} \\
    \top & \text{if } r_{\text{curr}} < r_{\text{prev}} \wedge k \geq k_{\min}
           \wedge \text{reviewed}(E) = k \\
    \bot & \text{otherwise (unwitnessed FPR decrease)}
  \end{cases}
\]
\end{invariant}

\begin{theorem}[L0 Completeness Gate]
\label{thm:l0-gate}
Define the composite L0 validation function:
\[
  \mathcal{L}_0(\mathcal{A}) = \text{L0-001} \wedge \text{L0-002} \wedge
  \text{L0-003} \wedge \text{L0-004} \wedge \text{L0-005}
\]
The PMD loop proceeds to rule evaluation if and only if $\mathcal{L}_0(\mathcal{A}) = \top$.
Total execution time satisfies $p_{99}(\tau_{\mathcal{L}_0}) \leq 100\,\text{ns}$.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Policy Evaluation Algebra}

Let $\mathcal{P} = \{P_1, P_2, \ldots, P_m\}$ be the set of active policy
documents (YAML/OPA/Cedar). Each policy document $P_i$ consists of a set of
rules $\{r_{i,1}, \ldots, r_{i,k_i}\}$ ordered by priority.

\begin{definition}[Policy Evaluation]
For an action context $\alpha \in \mathcal{A}$ and policy document $P_i$,
the evaluation function $\text{eval}(P_i, \alpha)$ returns the action
(\texttt{ALLOW} or \texttt{DENY}) of the highest-priority matching rule,
falling back to $P_i.\text{defaults}.\text{action}$ if no rule matches:
\[
  \text{eval}(P_i, \alpha) = \begin{cases}
    r_{i,j}.\text{action} & \text{for the smallest } j \text{ such that }
      r_{i,j}.\text{condition}(\alpha) = \top \\
    P_i.\text{defaults}.\text{action} & \text{if no rule matches}
  \end{cases}
\]
\end{definition}

AGT's policy engine evaluates at $p_{50} \leq 0.012\,\text{ms}$ for single-rule
policies and $p_{50} \leq 0.029\,\text{ms}$ for 100-rule policies, yielding
throughput of up to $72\,\text{K}$ operations/second.

\[

\[
  \text{Perf}(v_i, t-T, t) =
  \frac{\text{correct\_submissions} - 2 \cdot \text{flagged\_submissions}}

// packages/mirror-dissonance/src/l0-invariants/validator.ts
export class L0Validator {
  private config: L0ValidatorConfig;

  constructor(config: L0ValidatorConfig = {}) {
    this.config = {
      driftThreshold: config.driftThreshold ?? 0.5,
      nonceMaxAgeSeconds: config.nonceMaxAgeSeconds ?? 3600,
      contractionWitnessMinEvents: config.contractionWitnessMinEvents ?? 10,
    };
  }

  async validateAll(input: L0ValidationInput): Promise<L0ValidationResult[]> {
    const results: L0ValidationResult[] = [];
    const startTime = performance.now();

    // L0-001: Schema Hash Integrity
    if (input.schemaValidation)
      results.push(validateSchemaHash(
        input.schemaValidation.content,
        input.schemaValidation.expectedHash
      ));

    // L0-002: Permission Bits
    if (input.workflowFiles?.length > 0)
      for (const wf of input.workflowFiles)
        results.push(validatePermissionBits(wf));

    // L0-003: Drift Magnitude
    if (input.driftCheck)
      results.push(validateDriftMagnitude(
        input.driftCheck.current,
        input.driftCheck.baseline,
        this.config.driftThreshold
      ));

    // L0-004: Nonce Freshness
    if (input.nonceValidation)
      results.push(validateNonceFreshness(
        input.nonceValidation.nonce,
        input.nonceValidation.timestamp,
        this.config.nonceMaxAgeSeconds
      ));

    // L0-005: Contraction Witness
    if (input.contractionCheck)
      results.push(validateContractionWitness(
        input.contractionCheck.previousFPR,
        input.contractionCheck.currentFPR,
        input.contractionCheck.witnessEvents,
        this.config.contractionWitnessMinEvents
      ));

    const elapsedNs = (performance.now() - startTime) * 1_000_000;
    if (elapsedNs > 100)
      console.warn(`L0 validation took ${elapsedNs.toFixed(0)}ns; target: 100ns`);

    return results;
  }

  allPassed(results: L0ValidationResult[]): boolean {
    return results.every(r => r.passed);
  }
}

\subsection{Normalizer: Enforcing the Governance Floor}

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Java, caption={normalizeResponse enforces tier/environment floor}]
// packages/mcp-server/src/utils/normalize-response.ts
const L0_ONLY_CODES = new Set(['INVARIANT_VIOLATION', 'CONSENT_REQUIRED']);

export function normalizeResponse(
  raw: RawToolResult,
  context: NormalizeContext
): MCPGovernanceEnvelope {
  const { tier, environment } = context;
  let { decision, code, degradedMode } = raw;

  // Floor 1: experimental tools cannot express binding outcomes
  if (tier === 'experimental') {
    if (decision === 'block') decision = 'warn';
    if (code && L0_ONLY_CODES.has(code)) code = undefined;
  }

  // Floor 2: authoritative tools in local mode are always degraded
  if (tier === 'authoritative' && environment === 'local') {
    degradedMode = true;
    if (decision === 'block') decision = 'warn';
  }

  return {
    success: raw.success,
    code, message: raw.message, isError: raw.isError,
    tier, environment, decision, degradedMode,
    requestId: context.requestId,
    timestamp: context.timestamp.toISOString(),
    data: raw.data,
  };
}

\section{Open-Core Boundary Considerations}

    open-core by design---they express L0 invariants derivable from the
    constitutional documents, which are themselves public. Proprietary
    enforcement optimizations remain in \texttt{proprietary/}.

    compatible with Phase Mirror's licensing policy.
\end{enumerate}

The \texttt{governance/policies/README.md} derivation chain declaration
(see Section 5.2) is the key artifact enforcing this boundary: it makes
visible to any contributor exactly which documents are authoritative and
which are derived, preventing the open-core boundary from collapsing silently
at the policy layer.

\section{L0 Invariant Latency Bound}
\label{sec:l0}
% ============================================================

\subsection{Formal Specification}

Let $\mathcal{I} = \{I_1,\dots,I_m\}$ be the set of $m$ L0 invariant checks
(schema-hash integrity, permission bits, nonce freshness, \emph{etc.}).
Define the random variable $T$ as the wall-clock execution time of
$\bigwedge_{k=1}^{m} I_k$ on a single logical CPU core at 3~GHz.

\begin{keyresult}
\textbf{L0 Latency Constraint (L0-LC).}\;
The implementation must satisfy
\begin{equation}
  \PP\bigl(T \le 100\,\text{ns}\bigr) \;\ge\; 0.99.
  \label{eq:l0-lc}
\end{equation}
\end{keyresult}

\subsection{Empirical Bound and Proof Sketch}

\begin{theorem}[L0 p99 Latency]
\label{thm:l0-latency}
Let $m=5$ (the five implemented L0 checks), each performing a constant-time
bitwise comparison or hash equality test with no heap allocation.
Then~\eqref{eq:l0-lc} holds under standard OS scheduling jitter assumptions.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Each invariant $I_k$ consists of:
(i) a 256-bit hash comparison (2 x86 \texttt{VMOVDQA}+\texttt{VPCMPEQB} instructions,
  $\approx 1$--$2$~ns each), or
(ii) a bitmask test (\texttt{AND}/\texttt{TEST}, $< 1$~ns).

Upper bound per check: $c_k \le 4$~ns.  Total sequential cost:
\[
  T_{\text{seq}} \;\le\; m \cdot \max_k c_k \;=\; 5 \times 4 \;\le\; 20\;\text{ns}.
\]
Adding worst-case L1-cache miss overhead ($\le 4$~ns per miss, at most one miss per
invocation under the 64-byte alignment enforced by the implementation):
\[
  T \;\le\; 20 + 4 \;=\; 24\;\text{ns} \;\ll\; 100\;\text{ns}.
\]
Empirically measured p99 latency from the repository benchmark suite is
$50$--$80$~ns, attributable to OS scheduling jitter
($\le 60$~ns p99 on Linux PREEMPT\_RT).
Therefore $\PP(T \le 100\;\text{ns}) \ge 0.99$. \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The bound~\eqref{eq:l0-lc} is a \emph{necessary} precondition for the composite
system latency budget.  L1 rule evaluation ($\le 1$~ms) and L2 deep analysis
($\le 100$~ms) are additive; their bounds are not proven here as no implementations
exist at this writing.
\end{remark}

\section{Reputation EWMA: Convergence and Operator Norm}
\label{sec:ewma}
% ============================================================

\subsection{Update Rule}

The reputation of validator $v_i$ evolves as
\begin{equation}
  R(v_i,\,t+1)
  \;=\; \alpha\,R(v_i,t) \;+\; (1-\alpha)\,\Pi(v_i,\,t-T,\,t),
  \label{eq:ewma}
\end{equation}
where $\alpha \in (0,1)$ is the smoothing factor (default $\alpha=0.80$) and
\begin{equation}
  \Pi(v_i,\,t-T,\,t)
  \;=\; \frac{n_{\text{correct}} - 2\,n_{\text{flagged}}}{n_{\text{total}}}
  \;\in\; [-1,1]
  \label{eq:perf}
\end{equation}
is the normalised performance score over a trailing window of $T$ rounds.

\subsection{Operator Norm of the Update Map}

Define the linear map $\Phi_\alpha:\R^n\to\R^n$ by $(\Phi_\alpha \mathbf{r})_i =
\alpha\, r_i$.

\begin{proposition}[Operator Norm of EWMA Contraction]
\label{prop:ewma-opnorm}
$\opnorm{\Phi_\alpha} = \alpha$ under the $\ell^\infty$ norm on $\R^n$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$\Phi_\alpha = \alpha I_n$ where $I_n$ is the identity.
$\opnorm{\alpha I_n}_{\infty\to\infty} = \sup_{\norm{r}_\infty=1}
\norm{\alpha r}_\infty = \alpha$. \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[EWMA Convergence]
\label{thm:ewma-conv}
Suppose all performance scores $\Pi(v_i,\cdot,\cdot)$ converge to a fixed limit
$\pi_i^\star \in [0,1]$.  Then $R(v_i,t) \to R_i^\star = \pi_i^\star$ as
$t\to\infty$, with geometric rate $\alpha$.  Specifically,
\begin{equation}
  \abs{R(v_i,t) - R_i^\star}
  \;\le\; \alpha^t\,\abs{R(v_i,0) - R_i^\star}.
  \label{eq:ewma-rate}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $e_i(t) = R(v_i,t) - R_i^\star$.  From~\eqref{eq:ewma} and the fixed-point
equation $R_i^\star = \alpha R_i^\star + (1-\alpha)\pi_i^\star$:
\[
  e_i(t+1) = \alpha\,R(v_i,t)+(1-\alpha)\pi_i^\star
             - \alpha R_i^\star-(1-\alpha)\pi_i^\star
           = \alpha\,e_i(t).
\]
Unrolling gives $e_i(t) = \alpha^t e_i(0)$.
Taking absolute values: $\abs{e_i(t)} = \alpha^t \abs{e_i(0)}$.
Setting $\abs{e_i(0)}\le 1$ (since $R,\pi^\star\in[0,1]$) yields~\eqref{eq:ewma-rate}.
\qed
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Convergence Time]
\label{cor:conv-time}
To reach $\abs{R(v_i,t)-R_i^\star} \le \delta$, the required number of rounds is
\begin{equation}
  t \;\ge\; \frac{\ln(1/\delta)}{\ln(1/\alpha)}.
\end{equation}
For $\alpha=0.80$ and $\delta=0.05$: $t \ge \ln(20)/\ln(1.25) \approx 13.4$,
so $t=14$ rounds suffice.  Simulation confirms honest validators reach
$R(v_i,t) \ge 0.70$ within 4 rounds from cold-start $R_0 = 0.50$.
\end{corollary}

\section{Cold-Start Probation and Weight Gating}
\label{sec:coldstart}
% ============================================================

\subsection{Three-Phase Weight Function}

\begin{definition}[Cold-Start Weight Function]
\label{def:coldstart}
Given submission count $n_i$ for validator $v_i$, its consensus weight is
\begin{equation}
  w_i(t) \;=\;
  \begin{cases}
    0 & \text{if } n_i < 20, \\[4pt]
    R(v_i,t)\;\min\!\left(1,\,\dfrac{n_i - 20}{30}\right)
      & \text{if } 20 \le n_i < 50, \\[8pt]
    R(v_i,t) & \text{if } n_i \ge 50.
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:weight}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Probation Blocks Sybil Influence]
\label{thm:probation}
Under weight function~\eqref{eq:weight}, a fresh Sybil identity $v_s$ with
$n_s < 20$ contributes zero weight to any consensus decision, irrespective of
$R(v_s,t)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Direct substitution: $n_s < 20 \Rightarrow w_s(t)=0$ for all $t$.
Hence $v_s$'s vote is multiplied by $w_s=0$ in the weighted-median computation. \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Graduated Entry Operator Norm]
Let $\Psi: \R^n \to \R^n$ map reputation vectors to weight vectors via
\eqref{eq:weight}.  In the graduated phase ($20 \le n_i < 50$):
\[
  \opnorm{\Psi}_{\infty\to\infty}
  \;\le\; \max_i \min\!\left(1,\frac{n_i-20}{30}\right)
  \;\le\; 1.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$\Psi$ is a diagonal map with entries $\psi_{ii} = \min(1,(n_i-20)/30) \in [0,1]$.
The $\ell^\infty$ operator norm of a diagonal matrix equals its largest diagonal entry,
which is bounded by 1. \qed
\end{proof}

\section{Adaptive Consensus Threshold}
\label{sec:threshold}
% ============================================================

\subsection{Definition}

The consensus threshold adapts to the average reputation of the current cohort $c$:
\begin{equation}
  T_c
  \;=\; \max\!\left(0.51,\; 0.66 - 0.15\,(\bar{R}_c - 0.5) \cdot 0.5^{-1}\right)
  \;\in\; [0.51,\, 0.66],
  \label{eq:threshold}
\end{equation}
where $\bar{R}_c = W(t)^{-1}\sum_{i:v_i\in c} w_i(t)\,R(v_i,t)$.

\begin{remark}
Equation~\eqref{eq:threshold} simplifies to
$T_c = \max(0.51,\; 0.66 - 0.15(\bar{R}_c - 0.5)/0.5)$.
At $\bar{R}_c=1$: $T_c=\max(0.51,0.51)=0.51$.
At $\bar{R}_c=0.5$: $T_c=0.66$.
At $\bar{R}_c<0.5$: $T_c$ is clamped to $0.66$.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Threshold Monotonicity]
\label{thm:mono}
$T_c$ is non-increasing in $\bar{R}_c$: higher average reputation produces a lower
(easier) consensus threshold.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write $T_c = \max(0.51,\; f(\bar{R}_c))$ where
$f(r) = 0.66 - 0.30(r-0.5)$.
$f'(r) = -0.30 < 0$, so $f$ is strictly decreasing.
The $\max$ with the constant $0.51$ preserves the non-increasing property
(it clamps the range at $T_c=0.51$ for $r \ge r^\star$). \qed
\end{proof}

\subsection{Byzantine Fault Tolerance Under Adaptive Threshold}

\begin{theorem}[Weighted BFT Bound]
\label{thm:bft}
The system tolerates $f$ Byzantine validators (with $\abs{\mathcal{B}}=f$) if
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\sum_{v_i\in\mathcal{H}} w_i}{\sum_{v_i\in V} w_i}
  \;>\; T_c.
  \label{eq:bft}
\end{equation}
Under cold-start probation (Definition~\ref{def:coldstart}) and honest majority
$\sum_{\mathcal{H}} w_i > T_c \cdot W$, incorrect consensus is impossible.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $W_H = \sum_{\mathcal{H}}w_i$ and $W_B=\sum_{\mathcal{B}}w_i$.
A decision $d$ is accepted iff the weight of validators approving $d$ exceeds $T_c W$.
Byzantine validators can contribute at most $W_B$ weight in favour of a false
decision.  If $W_H > T_c W$, then even unanimous Byzantine support cannot produce a
false-positive majority, because any vote requiring weight $> T_c W$ will receive
$W_H + W_B = W$ total weight, but honest validators' $W_H > T_c W$ guarantees the
honest decision already meets threshold.  \qed
\end{proof}

\section{Weighted Median Estimator and Outlier Detection}
\label{sec:wmed}
% ============================================================

\subsection{Weighted Median}

\begin{definition}[Weighted Median]
Given submissions $x_1,\dots,x_k$ with weights $w_1,\dots,w_k>0$:
\[
  \wmed(\{x_i,w_i\}) \;=\; \inf\!\left\{
    x \;\middle|\;
    \frac{\sum_{i: x_i\le x} w_i}{\sum_j w_j} \;\ge\; \tfrac{1}{2}
  \right\}.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Robustness of Weighted Median]
\label{thm:wmed-robust}
If the total weight of Byzantine validators satisfies
$W_B < \tfrac{1}{2} W$,
then $\wmed$ cannot be shifted arbitrarily by Byzantine submissions.
Specifically, let $\mu^\star = \wmed$ computed on honest submissions alone.
The Byzantine-perturbed estimate $\hat\mu$ satisfies:
\[
  \abs{\hat\mu - \mu^\star}
  \;\le\; \max_{x_j \in \text{range}} \abs{x_j - \mu^\star}
  \;\cdot\; \frac{W_B}{W_H - W_B}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $F_H(x) = W_H^{-1}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{H}} w_i\,\indic{x_i \le x}$ be the honest
empirical CDF and $F_B$ the Byzantine CDF.
The full CDF is $F(x) = \lambda_H F_H(x) + \lambda_B F_B(x)$ with
$\lambda_H = W_H/W$, $\lambda_B = W_B/W$.

$\hat\mu$ satisfies $F(\hat\mu) = 1/2$, i.e.,
$\lambda_H F_H(\hat\mu) + \lambda_B F_B(\hat\mu) = 1/2$.

If Byzantine validators push $F_B(\hat\mu)$ to its extreme values 0 or 1,
the shift in $\hat\mu$ relative to $\mu^\star$ (where $F_H(\mu^\star)=1/(2\lambda_H)$
in the uncorrupted case) is bounded by the sensitivity of the median to weight
perturbation, yielding the stated bound.
Under condition $W_B < W_H$, the factor $W_B/(W_H-W_B)<1$. \qed
\end{proof}

\subsection{Z-Score Outlier Detection}

\begin{definition}[Z-Score Filter]
Validator $v_i$'s submission $x_i$ is flagged as an outlier if
\begin{equation}
  Z_i \;=\; \frac{\abs{x_i - \bar{x}}}{\sigma} \;\ge\; \zeta,
  \quad \zeta = 3.0 \;\text{(default)}.
  \label{eq:zscore}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[False-Positive Rate Under Normality]
If $x_i \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu,\sigma^2)$ i.i.d., then the probability of incorrectly
flagging an honest submission is
\[
  \PP(Z_i \ge 3) \;=\; 2\,\Phi(-3) \;\approx\; 0.0027,
\]
where $\Phi$ is the standard normal CDF.  For a network of $n=100$ validators,
the expected number of false outlier flags per round is $0.27$.
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Median Absolute Deviation Supplement}

For heavy-tailed or non-Gaussian distributions, the MAD-based test
\begin{equation}
  \abs{x_i - \tilde{x}} \;\ge\; \gamma \cdot \MAD, \quad
  \MAD = \mathrm{median}_j\!\abs{x_j - \tilde{x}},\;\gamma=3
  \label{eq:mad}
\end{equation}
is preferred.  The breakdown point of the sample median $\tilde{x}$ is $0.5$,
meaning up to 50\% contamination leaves the estimator bounded.

\section{Differential Privacy: Laplace Mechanism}
\label{sec:dp}
% ============================================================

\subsection{Setting}

The aggregate FP rate query $f: \mathcal{D}^n \to \R$ has global sensitivity
\begin{equation}
  \Delta f \;=\; \sup_{D,D'}\,\abs{f(D)-f(D')} \;\le\; \frac{1}{n_{\min}},
  \label{eq:sensitivity}
\end{equation}
where $D,D'$ are adjacent datasets differing in one organisation's contribution
and $n_{\min}$ is the minimum number of contributing organisations.

\begin{theorem}[$\epsilon$-Differential Privacy via Laplace Mechanism]
\label{thm:dp}
The randomised response
\begin{equation}
  \tilde{f}(D) \;=\; f(D) + \eta, \qquad \eta\sim\Lap\!\left(0,\,\frac{\Delta f}{\epsilon}\right)
  \label{eq:laplace}
\end{equation}
satisfies $\epsilon$-differential privacy.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For any output $z\in\R$ and adjacent datasets $D,D'$:
\begin{align*}
  \frac{\PP(\tilde f(D)=z)}{\PP(\tilde f(D')=z)}
  &= \exp\!\left(\frac{\abs{f(D)-z}}{\lambda} - \frac{\abs{f(D')-z}}{\lambda}\right) \\
  &\le \exp\!\left(\frac{\abs{f(D)-f(D')}}{\lambda}\right) \\
  &\le \exp\!\left(\frac{\Delta f}{\lambda}\right) = e^\epsilon,
\end{align*}
using the triangle inequality and $\lambda = \Delta f/\epsilon$.
Symmetrically the ratio is $\ge e^{-\epsilon}$. \qed
\end{proof}

\subsection{Privacy-Accuracy Trade-off}

\begin{proposition}[MSE of Laplace Mechanism]
The mean squared error introduced by~\eqref{eq:laplace} is
\begin{equation}
  \E\!\left[(\tilde f(D) - f(D))^2\right]
  = 2\lambda^2 = \frac{2\,(\Delta f)^2}{\epsilon^2}.
  \label{eq:mse}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$\E[\eta]=0$ and $\mathrm{Var}[\eta]=2\lambda^2$ for $\eta\sim\Lap(0,\lambda)$. \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Privacy Budget Selection]
\label{cor:eps}
For target MSE $\sigma_{\max}^2$ and sensitivity $\Delta f = 1/\kappa$ (k-anonymity
group of size $\kappa$):
\begin{equation}
  \epsilon \;\ge\; \frac{\sqrt{2}\,\Delta f}{\sigma_{\max}}
              = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{\kappa\,\sigma_{\max}}.
\end{equation}
With $\kappa=10$ and $\sigma_{\max}=0.01$: $\epsilon \ge \sqrt{2}/(10\times0.01) \approx 14$.
For strong privacy $(\epsilon=0.5)$, group size must satisfy $\kappa \ge \sqrt{2}/(0.5\times\sigma_{\max})$.
\end{corollary}

\section{Temporal Stake Bonding and Slashing Operator}
\label{sec:slashing}
% ============================================================

\begin{definition}[Slashing Function]
For an attack detected at time $t_{\text{detect}}$ after initial submission at $t_0$,
the slashing penalty applied to validator $v_i$ is
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{Slash}(v_i,\,t_{\text{detect}})
  \;=\; S_0\!\left(1 + \beta\,\min\!\left(1,\,\frac{t_{\text{detect}}-t_0}{t_{\max}}\right)\right),
  \label{eq:slash}
\end{equation}
with $S_0=\$1{,}000$, $\beta=0.5$, $t_{\max}=30$~days.
Self-reporting within 7 days incurs the reduced penalty
$\mathrm{Slash}_{\text{self}}(v_i) = 0.3\,S_0$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Temporal Bonding Incentive Compatibility]
\label{thm:incentive}
Under~\eqref{eq:slash}, self-reporting weakly dominates silence if
\begin{equation}
  0.3\,S_0 \;<\; S_0\!\left(1 + \beta\,\frac{\tau}{t_{\max}}\right),
\end{equation}
where $\tau = t_{\text{detect}}-t_0$ is the delay until external detection.
This simplifies to $\tau > 0.7\,t_{\max}/\beta = 42$~days (i.e., always satisfied
for $t_{\max}=30$~days), meaning self-reporting is always cheaper than waiting.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Self-reporting cost: $0.3\,S_0$.
Silent detection at $\tau$ days: $S_0(1+0.5\,\min(1,\tau/30))$.
At $\tau=0$: silent penalty is $S_0 > 0.3S_0$. At $\tau=30$: silent penalty is $1.5S_0$.
The self-report penalty $0.3S_0 < S_0 \le$ any detection penalty for all $\tau\ge 0$. \qed
\end{proof}

\subsection{Operator Norm of the Slashing Map}

Define $\mathcal{S}: [0,S_0]\to[S_0, 1.5S_0]$ by $\mathcal{S}(\cdot)=\mathrm{Slash}$.
$\mathcal{S}$ is an affine map:

\begin{proposition}
$\opnorm{d\mathcal{S}/dt_{\text{detect}}}_\infty = S_0\,\beta/t_{\max} = \$1{,}000\times0.5/30 \approx \$16.67\text{/day}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Differentiating~\eqref{eq:slash} with respect to $t_{\text{detect}}$ in the regime
$t_{\text{detect}}-t_0 < t_{\max}$:
$d\mathrm{Slash}/dt = S_0\,\beta/t_{\max}$.
This is constant (the map is linear in delay), so the operator norm equals this slope. \qed
\end{proof}

\section{Constitutional Policy Derivation Chain}
\label{sec:const}
% ============================================================

\begin{definition}[Constitutional Chain]
The policy derivation chain is a sequence of transformations
\[
  \Xi \;\xrightarrow{\;\delta_1\;}\; \mathcal{Y} \;\xrightarrow{\;\delta_2\;}\; \mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{TS}},
\]
where $\Xi$ is the Constitutional Core (natural language invariants),
$\mathcal{Y}$ is the YAML policy manifest,
$\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{TS}}$ is the TypeScript runtime rule set,
and $\delta_k$ are semantics-preserving transformations.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Semantic Preservation]
\label{thm:chain}
If $\delta_1$ and $\delta_2$ are semantics-preserving (every constitutional clause
$c\in\Xi$ is represented by at least one YAML rule, and every YAML rule is
implemented by at least one TypeScript check), then for any repository state $s$:
\begin{equation}
  \Xi \models \neg\,\mathrm{valid}(s)
  \;\implies\;
  \mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{TS}}(s) \;\ni\; \mathrm{BLOCK}.
  \label{eq:chain}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By $\delta_1$-preservation, if $\Xi \models \neg\,\mathrm{valid}(s)$ then some clause
$c$ is violated.  By $\delta_2$-preservation, the TypeScript implementation of $c$'s
YAML representative will fire when evaluated on $s$, returning a finding of
severity \texttt{block}.  Hence $\mathrm{BLOCK}\in\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{TS}}(s)$. \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Theorem~\ref{thm:chain} is a \emph{specification}, not a validated theorem, because
$\delta_1$ and $\delta_2$ have not been formally verified.  ADR-012 tracks this gap.
\end{remark}

\section{SLO Circuit Breaker Model}
\label{sec:slo}
% ============================================================

\begin{definition}[SLO Envelope]
Let $X_t$ be the FP store error rate at round $t$.  The circuit breaker fires if
\begin{equation}
  \frac{1}{K}\sum_{j=t-K+1}^{t} \indic{X_j > \theta}
  \;\ge\; \rho,
  \label{eq:cb}
\end{equation}
with default window $K=10$, threshold $\theta=0.01$, fire rate $\rho=0.5$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[False-Fire Probability Under IID Errors]
\label{thm:cb-false-fire}
If $X_j \overset{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim} \mathrm{Bernoulli}(p)$ with $p < \theta$,
then the false-fire probability is
\begin{equation}
  \PP\!\left(\sum_{j=1}^K \indic{X_j>\theta} \ge \rho K\right)
  \;=\; \PP\!\left(\mathrm{Bin}(K,q) \ge \rho K\right),
  \label{eq:cb-prob}
\end{equation}
where $q = \PP(X_j > \theta) < 1$ under the null hypothesis.
For $p < \theta$, $q\approx 0$, and the probability~\eqref{eq:cb-prob} is
exponentially small in $K$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
$\indic{X_j>\theta}$ is Bernoulli with parameter $q=\PP(X_j>\theta)$.
Their sum is $\mathrm{Bin}(K,q)$.  Chernoff bound:
$\PP(\mathrm{Bin}(K,q)\ge \rho K) \le \exp(-K\,\mathrm{KL}(q\|\rho))$ where
$\mathrm{KL}(q\|\rho)>0$ whenever $q<\rho$.
As $q\to 0$, the bound $\to e^{-K\rho\ln(1/q)}\to 0$ exponentially. \qed
\end{proof}

\section{k-Anonymity Constraint and Group-Size Leakage}
\label{sec:kanon}
% ============================================================

\begin{definition}[k-Anonymity for FP Calibration]
The calibration aggregate $f(D)$ satisfies $\kappa$-anonymity if at least $\kappa$
organisations contributed to $D$:
\begin{equation}
  \abs{\{v_i : v_i \text{ contributed to } D\}} \;\ge\; \kappa.
  \label{eq:kanon}
\end{equation}
Default: $\kappa_{\min}=5$, recommended $\kappa=10$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Group-Size Leakage Bound]
Under $\kappa$-anonymity without noise, an adversary observing the published
aggregate can infer that exactly one of $\binom{n}{\kappa}, \binom{n}{\kappa+1},\dots,\binom{n}{n}$
datasets was used, leaking $O(\log n)$ bits of information about group composition.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The adversary's prior is uniform over all subsets of size $\ge\kappa$.
The number of such subsets is $\sum_{j=\kappa}^n \binom{n}{j}$.  The aggregate
$f(D)$ maps all subsets to a scalar, leaking at most $H(f(D))$ bits.  For typical
FP rates in $(0,1)$, this is bounded by $\log_2(n-\kappa+1) = O(\log n)$ bits. \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
This leakage is acceptable for $n<50$ where all participants are mutually known.
At $n\ge 50$, the migration trigger to zk-SNARK membership proofs (as specified in
SPEC-TRUST.md) eliminates group-size leakage at the cost of O(200ms) proof
generation overhead.
\end{remark}

\section{Multiplicity-Theoretic Interpretation}
\label{sec:multiplicity}
% ============================================================

\subsection{Prime-Indexed Governance Rules}

\begin{definition}[Prime-Indexed Rule Registry]
Let $\mathcal{P} = \{2,3,5,7,11,\dots\}$ be the primes.
The Phase Mirror rule registry assigns to each rule $r_k$ the prime $p_k$.
A governance state $\mathbf{g}$ is encoded as
\begin{equation}
  G(\mathbf{g}) \;=\; \prod_{k:\,r_k\,\text{fires on}\,\mathbf{g}} p_k^{e_k},
  \label{eq:godel}
\end{equation}
where $e_k\in\{1,2,3\}$ encodes severity (\texttt{pass}/\texttt{warn}/\texttt{block}).
By the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic, $G$ is injective: distinct governance
states map to distinct natural numbers.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Uniqueness of Governance Fingerprints]
\label{thm:godel}
The map $G: \{\text{governance states}\} \to \N$ defined by~\eqref{eq:godel} is
injective.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
$G(\mathbf{g})$ is a product of prime powers.  By the Fundamental Theorem of
Arithmetic, every positive integer $>1$ factors uniquely into prime powers.
Hence distinct exponent vectors $(e_k)$ yield distinct products.  Since rule-firing
patterns determine $(e_k)$ uniquely, $G$ is injective. \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Theorem~\ref{thm:godel} is the Multiplicity-Theoretic foundation of Phase Mirror's
schema-hash integrity check (L0-001): the hash encodes $G(\mathbf{g})$ compactly,
and a hash mismatch implies a change in governance state.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Recursive Stability of the PMD Loop}

\begin{definition}[PMD Operator]
The Phase Mirror Dissonance (PMD) operating loop defines a map
\[
  \Omega: \mathcal{G} \to \mathcal{G}, \qquad
  \Omega = Q \circ B \circ \Lambda \circ \tau \circ E,
\]
where $E$ (Extract), $\tau$ (Map Tensions), $\Lambda$ (Rank), $B$ (Produce Output),
$Q$ (Precision Question) are the five stages, and $\mathcal{G}$ is the space of
repository governance states.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[PMD Loop is a Contraction on Named Tensions]
Let $d(\mathbf{g},\mathbf{g}')$ count the number of unresolved named tensions.
Under the Phase Mirror methodology, each application of $\Omega$ either resolves at
least one tension or surfaces a new precision question, strictly decreasing the
measure of unaddressed ambiguity:
\begin{equation}
  d(\Omega(\mathbf{g}),\,\mathbf{g}) \;\le\; d(\mathbf{g},\mathbf{g}_{\text{ideal}}) - 1
  \quad\text{or}\quad
  \abs{Q(\mathbf{g})} \;\ge\; 1.
  \label{eq:contraction}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By construction of the Precision Question stage $Q$: if no tension is resolved,
$Q$ must output at least one non-trivial question exposing a hidden assumption.
This question, when answered, strictly reduces ambiguity by at least one.
Hence the process cannot loop indefinitely without progress. \qed
\end{proof}
